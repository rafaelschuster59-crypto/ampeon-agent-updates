Research date: 21 Nov 2026.

## 1. Anthropic / Claude: "Keep thinking" (2025), Super Bowl 2026, later 2026 work

### Takeaway
Anthropic went from zero paid advertising to a Cannes Film Grand Prix in about nine months with one consistent platform ("Keep thinking", agency Mother): a positioning film for "problem solvers", a screen-free physical pop-up, then a comparative, humorous Super Bowl campaign built on a single product truth (no ads in Claude) that produced measurable download/usage lifts. The July 2026 follow-up ("Hard Questions") shows the limit: a darker, more abstract film polarised viewers.

### Cited Findings
**"Keep thinking" launch (Sept 2025)**
- First paid brand campaign for Claude, described as "multimillion-dollar"; creative by independent agency **Mother**, media by **Initiative**; production Love Song Films, first film directed by Daniel Wolfe; ran in international markets from 18 Sept 2025. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/09/18/anthropic-brand-campaign-claude) (A); [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/anthropic-launches-first-work-with-mother-for-ai-agent-claude/1933083) (A); [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/ai-assistant-claude-stars-in-anthropics-latest-campaign-via-mother-initiative/) (B)
- Positioning: Claude as the AI for **problem solvers**, for people who want AI to amplify rather than replace their thinking; head of brand marketing Andrew Stirk called it "a rallying cry and a promise". Film line reported as "There has never been a better time to have a problem". — [LS:N Global](https://www.lsnglobal.com/signals-stats/article/32462/claude-s-keep-thinking-campaign-positions-ai-agent-as-problem-solving-tool) (B); [The Stable](https://www.thestable.com.au/mother-anthropic-there-has-never-been-a-better-time-to-have-a-problem/) (B); [Creative Brief / Mother case study](https://www.creativebrief.com/agency/mother-london/case-studies/anthropic-a-rallying-cry-for-like-minded-problem-solvers-1) (A, agency primary)
- Reception (opinion): Creative Bloq called it "a refreshing take on AI branding". — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/creative-inspiration/advertising/claudes-new-ad-is-a-refreshing-take-on-ai-branding) (B)

**Pop-up (Oct 2025)**
- Anthropic took over Air Mail's West Village newsstand in New York for about a week as a screen-free "Zero Slop Zone": free coffee, books, pens, paper and baseball caps reading "thinking". — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-marketing-department-opens-zero-slop-zone-in-new-york/) (B); [TrendWatching](https://www.trendwatching.com/innovations/anthropics-zero-slop-pop-up-invites-new-yorkers-to-slow-down-and-think) (B)
- Results per Anthropic (self-reported): more than 5,000 visitors and about 10 million social impressions. — [Axios, 9 Oct 2025](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/09/anthropic-ibm-cursor-ai-popup-marketing) (A for reporting; figure is company-supplied)
- Critical view (opinion): nss magazine asked whether AI "only has to open a café to seem human". — [nss magazine DE](https://www.nssmag.com/de/lifestyle/46775/muss-ki-wirklich-nur-ein-cafe-eroffnen-um-menschlich-zu-wirken) (B)

**Super Bowl LX (Feb 2026): "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude."**
- Four films titled "Deception", "Betrayal", "Treachery", "Violation", two of them aired in the Super Bowl; none names OpenAI but all target OpenAI's plan to put ads into ChatGPT; campaign released 4 Feb 2026. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/09/what-was-anthropic-super-bowl-ad-chatgpt-therapy-sam-altman-reaction/) (A); [Famous Campaigns](https://www.famouscampaigns.com/2026/02/claude-campaign-imagines-a-dystopian-ad-filled-future/) (B)
- The aired spots are listed as "Can I Get a Six Pack Quickly?" and "How Can I Communicate Better with My Mom?"; scripts by Mother London, directed by Jeff Low through Biscuit Filmworks. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416145/day-5-cannes-lions-awards-two-claude-sb-ads-win-f.html) (B); [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/ads-are-coming-to-ai-but-not-to-claude) (B)
- Sam Altman's reaction: the ads are "funny, and I laughed", but "clearly dishonest"; he added that Anthropic "serves an expensive product to rich people" and that more Texans use ChatGPT for free than all US Claude users. — [TechCrunch, 4 Feb 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/04/sam-altman-got-exceptionally-testy-over-claude-super-bowl-ads) (A); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt/so-clearly-dishonest-sam-altman-responds-to-anthropics-super-bowl-ad-targeting-chatgpt-heres-what-he-said) (B); OpenAI also called the ad "deceptive" per [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/ai-rivalry-heats-up-ahead-of-super-bowl-as-openai-slams-anthropics-deceptive-ad/) (A)
- Commentary (opinion): Scott Galloway called it a "seminal moment", arguing a market leader should not reference the competition (i.e. Altman's reply amplified the challenger). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/02/09/super-bowl-ads-anthropic-openai-rivalry-trash-talk-ai-agent-war/) (A)

**Measured effects**
- Social sentiment (Meltwater): 25.5% of posts about Anthropic's ad were positive vs 16.3% for OpenAI's. — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/02/09/openai-super-bowl-spot-ai-bowl-anthropic) (B)
- EDO engagement ranking: the two Claude spots ranked 12th and 13th among Super Bowl ads; ai.com led. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ai-services-beat-beer-auto-brands-super-bowl-engagement) (B)
- App Store: Claude climbed from No. 41 to No. 7 on the US App Store by 13 Feb 2026, its best rank to that date. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3092849) (A); [Blockonomi](https://blockonomi.com/claude-ai-jumps-to-no-7-app-store-as-anthropic-blasts-openai-chatgpt-ads/) (C)
- Downloads (Appfigures estimates): about 148,000 US iOS+Android downloads Sunday to Tuesday after the game, up 32% from 112,000 in the prior three days; daily downloads above 170K on Super Bowl Sunday; downloads were already rising after the teasers. — [Appfigures](https://appfigures.com/resources/insights/claude-super-bowl-mobile-growth) (B, third-party estimate)
- Usage (BNP Paribas data via CNBC): daily active users +11%, site visits +6.5% after the ad. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/claude-users-grow-11--on-back-of-super-bowl-spot-anthropic-rides-wave-of-anti-ai-ads-sentiment) (B)
- Awards: the two Super Bowl spots won the **Film Grand Prix at Cannes Lions 2026** (plus a Gold and a Silver Lion); jury president Pelle Sjoenell called it "an ad about where ads shouldn't exist". — [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/cannes-lions-2026-all-the-grand-prix-winners/) (A); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416145/day-5-cannes-lions-awards-two-claude-sb-ads-win-f.html) (B)

**"Hard Questions" (July 2026)**
- Next iteration of "Keep thinking": film "There's hope in hard questions" (reported as 90 seconds by Campaign, two minutes elsewhere), Mother, director Myles McAuliffe; based on surveys of more than 120,000 people and 81,000 Claude users in 159 countries; companion site claude.com/hard-questions with audio experience and a form to submit questions. — [Campaign Ad of the Week](https://campaignlive.com/article/ad-week-anthropic-hard-questions-mother/1964334) (A); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/anthropic-claude-hard-questions-ad-mother) (A); [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/claude-owner-anthropic-invites-all-to-ask-hard-questions-in-new-work-via-mother-london/) (B)
- Campaign described the film as borrowing "from Apple's marketing playbook" while addressing public AI concerns. — [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/ad-week-anthropic-hard-questions-mother/1964334) (A)
- Reception was polarised: TechCrunch headline "Anthropic's newest ad is creeping people out" (weird imagery, doomer tone); Altman said he "thought it was satire"; Fast Company ME argued "advertising isn't the answer to Anthropic's hard questions". — [TechCrunch, 14 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/anthropics-newest-ad-is-creeping-people-out/) (A); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/claude/i-thought-it-was-satire-sam-altman-mocks-anthropics-latest-ai-ad-thats-getting-widely-blasted-for-being-dystopian-marketing-slop) (B); [Fast Company ME](https://fastcompanyme.com/co-design/advertising-isnt-the-answer-to-anthropics-hard-questions/) (B)

### Inferences
- The effective Anthropic work shares three traits a small brand can copy without budget: (1) one platform line held across every execution, (2) a claim rooted in a verifiable product/business fact rather than a mood ("no ads"), (3) a named enemy that is a practice, not a company ("ads in AI").
- The download/DAU lifts cannot be attributed cleanly to the ad: sources note the Opus 4.6 release and the pre-game teaser/PR fight (Altman's reply) happened in the same window. Treat "+32% downloads" as correlation with a plausible causal contribution.
- The comparative spot got much of its reach from the competitor's public response, i.e. earned media. That mechanism does not scale down automatically: a market leader rarely replies to a small unknown brand.
- "Hard Questions" suggests that when the same brand moves from concrete humour to abstract seriousness, reception gets riskier even with high craft.

### Gaps
- No System1 star ratings, iSpot or USA Today Ad Meter positions specific to the Claude spots were found (the System1 report page could not be opened).
- Media budget and Super Bowl spend for Anthropic: not found beyond "multimillion-dollar".
- OOH details of "Keep thinking" (formats, cities) and any DACH-specific flighting: not found. No DACH trade-press coverage (Werbewoche, persoenlich.com, Horizont, W&V) surfaced in searches.
- No campaign after July 2026 was found; absence of evidence, not proof that none exists.

## 2. OpenAI / ChatGPT

### Takeaway
OpenAI moved from a grand, abstract statement ("The Intelligence Age", 2025) to small everyday-use films shot on 35mm (autumn 2025) to a builder-focused Codex spot (Super Bowl 2026), mostly with an in-house team. Craft was praised, but testing data flagged weak branding in the everyday films, and in 2026 the earnest OpenAI spot generated less positive buzz than Anthropic's joke.

### Cited Findings
- **Super Bowl LIX (Feb 2025), "The Intelligence Age"**: OpenAI's first Super Bowl ad; 60-second black-and-white pointillist animation tracing human inventions from a single dot to AI; made by humans without generative video in the final film; cost reported at about $14m (The Verge reported $17m). — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/chatgpt-openai-super-bowl-ad/) (A); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/artificial-intelligence/openais-chatgpt-super-bowl-commercial-was-created-entirely-by-humans-and-maybe-we-dont-have-to-fear-ai-after-all) (B); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/global-affairs/introducing-the-intelligence-age/) (A, primary)
- Reception 2025 (opinion): Adweek called it "a pithy, pointillist chronicle of human ingenuity"; Inc. called it "the biggest Super Bowl upset". — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/chatgpt-openai-super-bowl-ad/) (A); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/jason-aten/openai-just-pulled-off-the-biggest-super-bowl-upset-of-all/91145798) (B)
- **Autumn 2025 ChatGPT brand campaign**: described as OpenAI's largest campaign to date; short films ("Pull-Up", "Dish", plus "Road Trip") showing everyday uses (workouts, recipes, trips); made by the in-house team with agency Isle of Any, directed by Miles Jay (Smuggler), shot on 35mm film; ChatGPT reportedly used as a brainstorming "co-creator". — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91411600/openai-first-major-brand-campaign?partner=rss) (B); [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/02/09/openai-super-bowl-spot-ai-bowl-anthropic) (B); [Zappi](https://www.zappi.io/web/blog/admiration-feature-chatgpts-pull-up/) (B)
- Positive view (opinion): Fast Company said "the music, casting, and product storytelling are pitch perfect". — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91411600/openai-first-major-brand-campaign?partner=rss) (B)
- Negative view and data: Adweek opinion piece "OpenAI Just Flunked Marketing 101"; in System1 testing only 59% of paid, fully attentive viewers knew what "Pull-Up" was advertising; iSpot ranked "Dish" lowest among tracked ChatGPT ads; Futurism: "new OpenAI ads are bad". — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/openai-just-flunked-marketing-101/) (A, opinion column); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/new-openai-ads-are-bad) (B); [iSpot](https://www.ispot.tv/hub/from-the-intelligence-age-to-family-farm-tracking-openais-creative-evolution/) (B)
- Zappi's test was more favourable: "Pull-Up" scored above norm on relevance (3.3 vs 3.1) and understanding (3.9 vs 3.4) and was better branded than "The Intelligence Age". — [Zappi](https://www.zappi.io/web/blog/admiration-feature-chatgpts-pull-up/) (B)
- **Super Bowl LX (Feb 2026)**: 60-second Codex-focused spot encouraging people to build with AI; made in-house; OpenAI's VP Global Creative described it as "the beginning of a builder's story". — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/openai-super-bowl-codex-commercial/) (A); [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/02/09/openai-super-bowl-spot-ai-bowl-anthropic) (B)
- Later 2026 context: Fortune reported an OpenAI influencer trip as a "comms disaster" (Aug 2026); a "QuitGPT" boycott movement was covered by TechRadar and Tom's Guide. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/06/openai-influencer-trip-comms-disaster/) (A); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/the-quitgpt-movement-is-targeting-chatgpt-with-a-boycott-and-spotlighting-the-politics-behind-the-ai-giant) (B)

### Inferences
- The 59% recognition figure is the most transferable lesson: beautiful, quiet, filmic everyday stories fail if the product and brand are not unmistakable early and throughout. Small brands with low base awareness are more exposed to this than ChatGPT.
- The two testing vendors disagree (System1/iSpot negative, Zappi positive), so "the 35mm campaign failed" is not established; "branding/attribution was its weak point" is the defensible reading.

### Gaps
- No Ad Meter or System1 star rating for "The Intelligence Age" or the 2026 Codex spot was found.
- Details of the Fortune "influencer trip" and of "Family Farm" (named in the iSpot headline) were not readable.

## 3. X / xAI / Grok and Tesla: founder-led earned media

### Takeaway
X and xAI advertise little in the classic sense and lean on Musk's personal reach, product stunts and contests; documented paid brand work is thin and poorly sourced. Tesla is the long-running example of near-zero advertising working while the founder is an asset, and of the same dependence becoming a liability when the founder's public persona turns off buyers.

### Cited Findings
- X was reported to be planning its **first marketing campaign since Musk's 2022 takeover**, including promotion of X Money. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/x-plans-marketing-campaign-musk-apple-resumes) (B)
- Super Bowl 2026: instead of a conventional spot, X ran "Brand Ranx", a Grok-powered live ranking of the most talked-about ads, and a $1m contest for Grok-generated video to form part of its game-day promotion. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/02/09/x-super-bowl-ad-rankings-brandranx) (A); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-offers-million-dollar-prize-grok-super-bowl-ad-video-generation/811501) (B); critical take "AI slop ad just won $1 million": [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/grok-ai-ad-contest) (B)
- Reports of paid Grok ads on Instagram Reels, YouTube pre-roll and Reddit (Sept 2026) and a Grok display on the Las Vegas Sphere (Nov 2026) come from a single vendor blog and are unconfirmed. — [Basenor blog](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/grok-is-everywhere-5-details-behind-xais-ad-push) (C)
- Grok's relation to advertising is mainly as an ad product: Musk said ads inside Grok answers would "pay for those expensive GPUs". — [Fortune, 8 Aug 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/08/08/elon-musk-grok-ai-ads/) (A)
- X ad revenue: $1.84bn in 2025 per SpaceX filings cited by Hollywood Reporter; eMarketer estimated $2.26bn. Sources conflict. — [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/elon-musk-spacex-media-ambitions-x-advertising-starlink-1236602996) (A); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/x-plans-marketing-campaign-musk-apple-resumes) (B)
- **Tesla**: historically reported $0 advertising; in May 2023 Musk said "We'll try a little advertising and see how it goes". — [Fortune, 16 May 2023](https://fortune.com/2023/05/16/elon-musk-tesla-cars-ads-commercials/) (A)
- Tesla's Q1 2024 US digital ad spend was 900x the prior year (Sensor Tower); WSJ/Vivvix estimated about $6.4m digital for the prior year; the marketing team was reportedly laid off in April 2024. — [Fortune, 29 Mar 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/03/29/tesla-scraps-elon-musk-advertising-stock/) (A)
- Tesla spent $400,000 on X ads in 2024 and $10,000 in the first two months of 2025. — [TechCrunch, 5 Sep 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/05/teslas-ad-spend-on-x-has-shrunk-to-almost-nothing/) (A)
- Founder risk: Tesla sales in Europe (EU, EFTA, UK) fell 28% in the first 11 months of 2025 while European EV sales rose 27%. — [Statista](https://statista.com/chart/34036/tesla-in-europe) (B)
- Attribution is contested: Fortune reported analysts saying it was too early to isolate Musk's effect from other factors; NBC reported brand value and reputation declining "due largely" to Musk's rhetoric. — [Fortune, 3 Mar 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/03/elon-musks-embrace-of-right-wing-politics-is-turning-off-potential-customers-experts-warn-its-marketing-101/) (A); [NBC](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/business/money-report/teslas-europe-sales-plunge-49-on-brand-damage-rising-competition/3709460/) (A)

### Inferences
- Founder-led earned media substitutes for paid reach only when the founder already has a very large audience; it concentrates brand risk in one person and one channel. For Ampeon, founder-led content is a cost-efficient distribution layer, not a substitute for a brand that stands without the founder.
- The Grok contest shows the cheap-reach route (user-generated AI video) trades away craft, which is the opposite of an Apple-style approach.

### Gaps
- **"You're the media now"**: no reliable source found for an X brand campaign under this line; cannot be confirmed as a paid campaign from this research.
- No verified spend figures for X or xAI brand marketing. Grok consumer ad claims rest on one low-reliability blog.

## 4. Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, Apple Intelligence: what worked, what backfired

### Takeaway
The same companies produced both the best-liked and the most-criticised AI ads; the dividing line is whether AI supports a person in something they still do themselves, or replaces an act people value doing (a child's fan letter, creative tools), or promises features that do not ship.

### Cited Findings
- **Google "Dear Sydney" (Paris Olympics, summer 2024)**: a father asks Gemini to help his daughter write a fan letter to Sydney McLaughlin-Levrone; after criticism (outsourcing a child's sincere expression, "lazy parenting") Google first defended it, then said "given the feedback, we have decided to phase the ad out of our Olympics rotation". — [Axios, 2 Aug 2024](https://www.axios.com/2024/08/02/olympics-google-gemini-ad-dear-sydney) (A); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/08/02/google-chatbot-artificial-intelligence-gemini-advertisement-olympics-dear-sydney/) (A)
- Notably, System1 testing gave "Dear Sydney" 4.4 out of 5.9 stars, i.e. it tested well with general audiences despite the public backlash. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/08/02/google-chatbot-artificial-intelligence-gemini-advertisement-olympics-dear-sydney/) (A, citing Business Insider/System1)
- **Apple "Crush!" (iPad Pro, May 2024)**: hydraulic press destroying instruments and paint; Apple apologised and pulled it from TV. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/08/02/google-chatbot-artificial-intelligence-gemini-advertisement-olympics-dear-sydney/) (A)
- **Google "Dream Job" (Super Bowl LIX, Feb 2025)**: a father uses Gemini Live on Pixel 9 to prepare for a job interview, drawing on what parenting taught him; coverage called it emotional and said Google "redeems Gemini after awkward Olympics ad". Production credit conflicts: Adweek says in-house, LBB credits Smuggler. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/googles-heartfelt-super-bowl-ad-pixel/) (A); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/google-redeems-gemini-after-awkward-olympics-ad-in-a-super-bowl-spot-among-many-sentimental-ai-contenders) (B); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/gemini-live-follows-wherever-the-conservations-takes-you-in-google-pixel-super-bowl-spot) (A)
- **Super Bowl LX (Feb 2026), System1**: AI-focused spots averaged 2.1 stars vs 2.7 for all ads; AI was 18% of ads in System1's count (iSpot: 15 of 66 ads, 23%). Google's Gemini spot about a mother helping her son through a house move scored 3.3 stars, the report's positive example, because AI was embedded in a human story. System1's press headline: "AI Killed Big Game Ad Effectiveness; Human-Centric Stories Won the Night". — [System1](https://system1group.com/blog/did-ai-kill-the-super-bowl-star) (A, vendor primary); [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260209998644/en/AI-Killed-Big-Game-Ad-Effectiveness-Human-Centric-Stories-Won-the-Night) (A); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/en/article/system1s-returns-are-in-how-2026s-super-bowl-ads-scored-or-didnt-with-consumers-749a7d5fb602438896d64623b131c286) (A)
- Trade framing 2026: Adweek "Super Bowl revealed AI messaging crisis"; Rolling Stone: viewers "weren't buying it". — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brandmarketing/super-bowl-revealed-ai-messaging-crisis) (A); [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/super-bowl-ai-ads-critics-experts-1235514369/) (A)
- **Microsoft Copilot**: Super Bowl 2024 "Watch Me" was Microsoft's first Super Bowl ad since 2020, timed to a Copilot redesign; Adweek critics: shows "Copilot's power but not its personality"; the app climbed app-store charts afterwards. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/02/13/microsoft-copilot-super-bowl-ad) (A); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/super-bowl-hot-take-microsoft-shows-copilots-power-but-not-its-personality/) (A); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/microsoft-copilot-ai-climbs-google-apple-app-store-charts-after-super-bowl-ad-despite-errors) (B)
- Super Bowl 2026 Copilot spot (NFL scouts using Copilot in Excel, an extension of an existing campaign): Adweek's creative panel said it "failed to deliver" and overpromised the use case; context given: about 15m paid Copilot seats out of 450m Microsoft 365 seats (about 3.3%). — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/creatives-react-in-ai-heavy-super-bowl-microsoft-copilot-ad-failed-to-deliver/) (A)
- **Perplexity (March 2025)**: 90-second spot with Squid Game star Lee Jung-jae, who fails with fictional search engine "Poogle" and succeeds with Perplexity, including a nod to Google's "glue on pizza" error; reported spend $5m+; part of a year-long partnership with Artist United. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/03/19/perplexity-takes-google-and-chatgpt-with-its-boldest-move-yet-it-enough) (A); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/perplexity-new-ad-pokes-fun-google-ai) (B); [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/perplexity-just-brought-in-a-squid-game-star-to-convince-you-to-ditch-google) (B)
- **Apple Intelligence**: the iPhone 16 ad with Bella Ramsey showing a "more personal Siri" (launched Sept 2024) was pulled in March 2025 when Apple delayed the feature; the US National Advertising Division found "Available Now" claims misleading and Apple agreed to discontinue the ad; a false-advertising lawsuit followed. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/03/07/apple-pulls-ad-that-showed-siri-being-useful-after-delaying-feature-that-could-make-siri-useful/) (B); [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/03/08/apple-pulls-bella-ramsey-ad-that-promoted-vaporware-personalized-siri-feature) (B, expert blog); [NBC on NAD](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/apple-ai-ads-did-not-make-adequate-disclosures-watchdog-says/3809840/) (A)

### Inferences
- "Dear Sydney" testing well in System1 while being pulled shows that general-audience pre-testing does not capture backlash from a vocal minority (writers, parents, creatives) who drive the public narrative. For a meeting-listener product the equivalent vocal group is privacy-sensitive people.
- The Apple Siri case is the most relevant warning for a small software vendor: only show what the shipping build does today. Even Apple was sanctioned and sued for demoing unreleased capability.
- Perplexity's comparative humour against a fictional stand-in ("Poogle") is a way to do comparison without naming the rival; in Switzerland an identifiable competitor still falls under comparative-advertising rules (see section 7).

### Gaps
- System1/Ad Meter scores for "Dream Job" and for the individual 2026 AI spots other than Gemini (3.3) were not found.
- No Copilot consumer campaign for 2025 documented in the results.

## 5. Patterns across successful and failed AI ads

### Takeaway
Across 2024 to 2026 the evidence points the same way: ads that put a person in the lead with a concrete, modest use case and visible warmth or humour outperform ads that sell AI itself, and backlash clusters around three triggers.

### Cited Findings
- Quantified pattern: AI-themed Super Bowl 2026 ads averaged 2.1 System1 stars vs 2.7 overall; the best AI example (Gemini, 3.3) worked by integrating AI into a human story. — [System1](https://system1group.com/blog/did-ai-kill-the-super-bowl-star) (A)
- Concrete use case over abstraction: Zappi found the everyday "Pull-Up" better branded and more relevant than the abstract "Intelligence Age"; but 59% product recognition in System1 shows a quiet use-case film still needs clear branding. — [Zappi](https://www.zappi.io/web/blog/admiration-feature-chatgpts-pull-up/) (B); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/openai-just-flunked-marketing-101/) (A)
- Humour plus a clear single claim: Anthropic's Super Bowl spots led OpenAI's on positive sentiment (25.5% vs 16.3%) and won the Cannes Film Grand Prix. — [Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2026/02/09/openai-super-bowl-spot-ai-bowl-anthropic) (B); [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/cannes-lions-2026-all-the-grand-prix-winners/) (A)
- Backlash trigger 1, replacing valued human effort: "Dear Sydney" pulled. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/08/02/olympics-google-gemini-ad-dear-sydney) (A)
- Backlash trigger 2, destroying or belittling human craft: Apple "Crush!" apology. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/08/02/google-chatbot-artificial-intelligence-gemini-advertisement-olympics-dear-sydney/) (A)
- Backlash trigger 3, overpromising: Apple's Siri ad pulled, NAD ruling, lawsuit; Copilot 2026 criticised for overpromising the shown use case. — [NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/apple-ai-ads-did-not-make-adequate-disclosures-watchdog-says/3809840/) (A); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/creatives-react-in-ai-heavy-super-bowl-microsoft-copilot-ad-failed-to-deliver/) (A)
- Backlash trigger 4, ominous or abstract tone about AI itself: "Hard Questions" described as creeping people out. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/anthropics-newest-ad-is-creeping-people-out/) (A)
- Physical, screen-free experiences were used by several AI brands in 2025 to build trust (Anthropic, IBM, Cursor). — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/09/anthropic-ibm-cursor-ai-popup-marketing) (A)

### Inferences
- Working checklist for an Ampeon film derived from the above: the user speaks and decides, the app listens and prepares; one situation (e.g. after a client call, the follow-up e-mail draft is waiting); product and name visible within the first seconds and at the end; no feature shown that is not in the current release; no scene where the app replaces a personal message the sender would be expected to write themselves.
- For a "listener" product the "replacing human effort" trap has a specific form: an ad that implies you no longer need to pay attention in meetings will read as disrespect to the other participants. "You stay present, it takes the notes" is the safe framing.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or large-sample study isolating "human in the lead" as the causal variable; the pattern rests on vendor testing and case evidence.

## 6. Small-brand evidence: effectiveness research and small-company examples

### Takeaway
The effectiveness literature says small brands grow by reaching future buyers before they are in market and by being emotionally distinctive rather than dull; the best-documented small software examples (Linear, Arc, Granola, Nothing) reached scale with very low paid spend by concentrating on product craft, founder/video storytelling and community. The evidence base for those examples is largely interviews and third-party write-ups, not audited numbers.

### Cited Findings
**Research**
- Binet & Field, "The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing" (LinkedIn B2B Institute / IPA, 2019): invest in share of voice, balance brand and activation, expand the customer base, maximise mental availability, harness emotion. — [IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing/) (A); [LinkedIn B2B Institute PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf) (A, primary)
- Share-of-voice rule: brands with SOV above market share tend to grow; one trade summary quantifies roughly 10 points of excess SOV as 0.7 points of share growth. — [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/en-uk/marketing-solutions/blog/posts/B2B-Marketing/2020/The-B2B-Marketers-Guide-To-The-Share-of-Voice-Rule) (A)
- Brand/activation split: Binet & Field's general optimum is about 60:40 brand to activation (62:38 in "Effectiveness in Context", 2018). For brands in their first year Peter Field's analysis found the optimum reversed to about 35% brand, 65% performance. — [IPA blog](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/if-you-market-like-a-smaller-brand-you-will-become-a-smaller-brand/) (A); [The7stars](https://www.the7stars.co.uk/effs-sake-looking-latest-report-binet-field/) (B)
- Caveats: the IPA databank consists of awards entries and is not a representative sample; Byron Sharp called the 60:40 principle "terrible and misleading". — [IPA review](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/effectiveness-in-context-reviewed-by-michael-lee/) (A); [Mi3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/07-09-2022/how-brands-shrink-prof-byron-sharps-takedown-binet-field-attention-metrics) (B)
- 95:5 rule (Prof. John Dawes, Ehrenberg-Bass, with LinkedIn B2B Institute, 2021): at any time only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market because categories are bought infrequently (e.g. providers changed roughly every five years); Dawes says the figure is a heuristic, not a precise law. Implication: most advertising reaches people who will buy later, so it works by building memory. — [Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/the-955-rule-why-b2b-growth-starts-long-before-the-purchase) (A, primary); [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule) (A); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule) (A)
- System1 / Peter Field / eatbigfish, "The Extraordinary Cost of Dull": neutrality is the most common response to US ads (47%); Field's IPA analysis found dull campaigns need about 2.6x the media spend of interesting ones for the same very large share growth; System1 models $189bn extra US media spend needed by dull ads to match non-dull ones (a modelled estimate from Nielsen TV spend, not a measured loss). — [System1](https://system1group.com/blog/the-extraordinary-cost-of-dull) (A, vendor primary); [Westwood One summary](https://www.westwoodone.com/blog/2025/01/27/the-extraordinary-cost-of-dull-boring-ads-require-2x-to-2-6x-greater-media-spend-to-achieve-same-impact-as-interesting-ads/) (B); [Contagious interview with Adam Morgan](https://contagious.com/news-and-views/adam-morgan-on-the-cost-of-dull-advertising) (A)
- Adam Morgan, "Eating the Big Fish" (1999, 2nd ed. 2009): introduced "challenger brand"; challenger is a mindset, not a market rank; credos include breaking with the immediate past, building a "lighthouse identity", assuming thought leadership of the category, and sacrifice. Morgan: challengers are defined by "what you're challenging, not who". — [eatbigfish](https://www.eatbigfish.com/books/eating-the-big-fish) (A, primary); [CMO Australia](https://www.cmo.com.au/article/print/609965/challenger-brands-about-what-re-challenging-who-says-adam-morgan/) (B)
- Counterpoint (opinion): Andrew Tindall (System1), after debating Les Binet, argues small brands first need a compelling reason to be chosen before scaling reach. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/andrew-tindall-what-my-debate-with-les-binet-reveals-about-challenger-brands) (A, opinion)

**Company examples**
- **Linear**: third-party analysis claims about $35,000 total paid marketing from founding (2019) to its Series C; growth attributed to product quality, word of mouth, founder-led posting by Karri Saarinen and deliberate pursuit of admired customer logos; brand and website treated as evolving products. Linear now advertises a Demand Generation Lead role owning paid media, so "no paid ads" no longer holds. — [Ideaplan](https://www.ideaplan.io/blog/how-linear-grew-to-1-billion-with-35k-marketing-spend) (C, figure unverified); [First Round Review](https://review.firstround.com/linears-path-to-product-market-fit/) (A, founder interview); [Linear careers](https://linear.app/careers/910805ac-96e8-42b9-a8e9-8adf4c4ef536) (A, primary)
- **The Browser Company (Arc)**: founder Josh Miller speaking to a webcam, explaining a handful of features in about five minutes and voicing honest doubt; a dedicated three-person "Storytelling" team making narrative video for non-users; YouTube channel deliberately not how-to or hype promos; philosophy of optimising for how users feel. — [Failory](https://newsletter.failory.com/p/lights-camera-arction) (B); [Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/the-browser-company-video) (B); [Every](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/the-browser-company-is-building-a-brand-that-drives-viral-growth) (B); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/josh-miller) (A, founder interview)
- **Granola** (closest category analogue to Ampeon Agent: Mac-first AI meeting notes): spread among VCs and founders largely by word of mouth; $43m at $250m valuation (May 2025), then $125m Series C at $1.5bn (25 Mar 2026, Index Ventures lead); expanding from notetaker to enterprise context platform. — [TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/14/ai-note-taking-app-granola-raises-43m-at-250m-valuation-launches-collaborative-features) (A); [TechCrunch 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/granola-raises-125m-hits-1-5b-valuation-as-it-expands-from-meeting-notetaker-to-enterprise-ai-app/) (A); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-25/ai-notetaker-granola-hits-1-5-billion-value-in-125-million-funding) (A)
- **Nothing**: community-first hype model carried over from OnePlus; $1.5m community investment round sold out in 54 seconds; gated pre-order passes; brand chief says the company lacks Apple/Samsung budgets and spends on smaller local projects aimed at core customers, rejecting "boring and generic" phone ads. — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/nothing-community-carl-pei-interview-1227393) (B); [Fortune, 20 Oct 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/10/20/nothings-brand-chief-is-fed-up-of-boring-and-generic-smartphone-ads-he-shares-his-marketing-strategy-for-taking-on-samsung-and-apple/) (A)

### Inferences
- Field's first-year finding (about 35% brand, 65% activation) and the 95:5 rule are not in conflict for Ampeon: early on, most effort goes to converting the few in-market Mac users (search, Product Hunt, comparison content, trial flow), while a smaller but constant share builds memory among those who are not yet looking.
- With a tiny budget, share of voice cannot be bought across a whole category; the practical translation is to narrow the category until meaningful share of voice is affordable (e.g. German-speaking Mac professionals in Switzerland) and to be non-dull, since the "cost of dull" multiplier (about 2.6x) is the one lever that does not require money.
- The small-company examples share an "Apple-like" trait that is cheap: restraint and consistency (one typeface, one tone, product shown plainly, few claims), plus a human voice explaining why the product exists. None of them began with broadcast advertising.
- Survivorship bias is strong in these cases; they show what is possible, not what is typical.

### Gaps
- **No sources found in this session** for Cultured Code (Things), Superhuman, Notion, Raycast or Teenage Engineering marketing approaches or budgets; they should not be cited in the report unless separately sourced.
- Linear's $35k figure is from a secondary blog and unverified.
- Granola's brand/launch creative (films, design language) was not documented in the results, only funding and word-of-mouth growth.
- The original Dawes report and the full Binet & Field B2B report were not read in full; only summaries.

## 7. Practical constraints and channels for a small Swiss/DACH B2B/prosumer software brand

### Takeaway
Legal guardrails could be partly sourced: Swiss law permits comparative advertising but prohibits comparisons that are inaccurate, misleading, unnecessarily disparaging or unnecessarily imitative, and recording a non-public conversation without the consent of all participants is a criminal offence prosecuted on complaint, which directly constrains what a meeting-listener may claim or show. Channel and production-cost guidance could not be sourced in this session and is given only as inference.

### Cited Findings
- Swiss UWG Art. 3 para. 1 lit. e covers comparative advertising; the sub-cases are misleading statements, disparaging ("unnötig verletzende") statements and unnecessarily imitative ("unnötig anlehnende") advertising; lit. e takes precedence over the general misleading-statement rule in lit. b where competitor products are concerned. — [Universität Zürich, Falllösung](https://ius.uzh.ch/dam/jcr:ffffffff-ed6d-5732-ffff-ffff843a0ed5/Loesung_Fall_3.pdf) (A, academic teaching material); [UWG text, admin.ch](https://www.admin.ch/opc/de/classified-compilation/19860391/201407010000/241.pdf) (A, primary; 2014 consolidated version, check current Fedlex text)
- Lit. e is described as important in Swiss advertising practice. — [Chambers Advertising & Marketing Guide, Switzerland](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/advertising-marketing-2023/switzerland) (A)
- Art. 179ter StGB: a participant who records a non-public conversation without the consent of the other participants is liable to custodial sentence of up to one year or a fine; storing, evaluating or making such a recording accessible is also punishable; the offence is prosecuted on complaint. — [RecordingLaw.com (DE)](https://www.recordinglaw.com/de/switzerland/recording-law/recording-conversations/) (C, non-official summary); [EDÖB schematic overview on recording conversations](https://WWW.EDOEB.ADMIN.CH/dam/edoeb/de/Dokumente/datenschutz/ds01_02_06b%20Aufzeichnung%20von%20Gespr%C3%A4chen%20-%20schematische%20%C3%9Cbersicht_DE.pdf.download.pdf/ds01_02_06b%20Aufzeichnung%20von%20Gespr%C3%A4chen%20-%20schematische%20%C3%9Cbersicht_DE.pdf) (A, federal data protection commissioner)
- The EDÖB requires consent to be as clear and transparent as possible regarding both the recording and its purpose; data-protection claims (including destruction of the recording) arise under Art. 32 DSG. — [EDÖB overview](https://WWW.EDOEB.ADMIN.CH/dam/edoeb/de/Dokumente/datenschutz/ds01_02_06b%20Aufzeichnung%20von%20Gespr%C3%A4chen%20-%20schematische%20%C3%9Cbersicht_DE.pdf.download.pdf/ds01_02_06b%20Aufzeichnung%20von%20Gespr%C3%A4chen%20-%20schematische%20%C3%9Cbersicht_DE.pdf) (A)
- Regulatory precedent on AI feature claims: Apple had to drop "Available Now" claims for Apple Intelligence features after a US NAD review and faces a false-advertising suit. — [NBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/apple-ai-ads-did-not-make-adequate-disclosures-watchdog-says/3809840/) (A)

### Inferences
- **Comparative advertising**: an Anthropic-style "not like them" message is possible in Switzerland if every comparative fact is true, provable and not gratuitously demeaning. The lower-risk variant is to oppose a practice ("cloud bots that join your call", "notes that leave your Mac") rather than a named competitor, which also matches Adam Morgan's "what, not who".
- **Recording/privacy in creative**: marketing should not depict or imply covert listening. Any scene showing a meeting should show (or state) that participants know. Claims such as "local", "stays on your Mac", "Swiss-hosted" or "no recording stored" must match the actual data flow exactly, since under UWG they are factual claims and under DSG they describe processing of personal data. The word "Zuhörer" is warm but needs the consent context next to it.
- **Germany/Austria**: German law (§ 201 StGB, § 6 UWG) differs from Swiss law; search results flagged that German UWG commentary is only partly transferable. A DACH rollout needs a separate check.
- **Channels (unsourced judgement)**: for a Mac-only prosumer tool with a small budget the realistic set is an owned launch film on the website and YouTube, founder-led LinkedIn in German, Product Hunt and Mac-centric communities/newsletters for the in-market 5%, and small retargeting budgets on LinkedIn/Meta/YouTube; X is weak for German-speaking Swiss B2B reach. This follows from the 95:5 logic and the Arc/Linear cases, not from DACH channel data.
- **Production (unsourced judgement)**: the Arc pattern (founder to camera, real screen capture, one idea per video, honest tone) and the Apple pattern (product on plain background, one sentence per frame, real UI) are both achievable with a single-operator setup; the craft lies in restraint, sound and editing rather than budget.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on DACH/Swiss channel costs, reach or benchmarks (LinkedIn CPMs in CH, YouTube reach among Swiss Mac users), and no DACH trade-press material (Werbewoche, persoenlich.com, Horizont, W&V) was retrieved.
- No sourced cost ranges for low-budget product films.
- No EDÖB or cantonal guidance specific to AI notetakers/meeting bots was found; no EU AI Act transparency analysis was retrieved.
- Current Fedlex wording of UWG Art. 3 and StGB Art. 179ter was not read directly; one secondary source gave a conflicting maximum penalty (three years, probably Art. 179bis). Legal points here are orientation only and need review by Swiss counsel.
- Whether Ampeon Agent records audio, transcribes live without storing audio, or joins calls as a visible participant is unknown (site unreachable); the legal framing depends on this.
