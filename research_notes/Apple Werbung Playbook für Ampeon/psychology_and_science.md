# Psychology and Science of Emotional / Brand Advertising and Consumer Attachment to Apple

**How to read these notes**

Reliability rating per finding:
- **[A]** peer-reviewed journal article / meta-analysis
- **[B]** institute or industry dataset with published method (IPA, Ehrenberg-Bass, Nielsen, Kantar, CIRP, Counterpoint) — real data, but often commercially funded, self-selected or not peer-reviewed
- **[C]** trade press, vendor claims, books by practitioners, secondary reporting
- **[D]** weak / anecdotal / single-case / contested to the point of being unusable as evidence

- **(V)** = found and checked via web search/fetch in this research session (Nov 2026)

## 1. What does the effectiveness research say about emotional vs. rational advertising and brand-building vs. activation?

### Takeaway
The industry-effectiveness literature (Binet & Field/IPA, System1, Ehrenberg-Bass, Nielsen, Kantar) converges on: broad-reach, emotional, distinctive, consistently branded advertising builds long-term demand and pricing power, while rational/targeted activation harvests short-term sales; creative quality is the biggest lever an advertiser controls. However, this evidence is mostly non-peer-reviewed, drawn from award entries or vendor databases, and the strongest academic econometric work (Shapiro, Hitsch & Tuchman 2021) finds much smaller average advertising effects — so "60/40" is a heuristic, not a law.

### Cited Findings

**Les Binet & Peter Field / IPA**
- *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013) analyses the IPA Effectiveness Databank (c. 996 campaigns, c. 700 brands, 30 years of award case studies). Core claims: short-term activation produces sales spikes that decay; brand-building effects accumulate and dominate after roughly 6 months; the two require different creative and media. [B] (NR for sample size) — [IPA presentation deck](https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf); [Thinkbox summary](https://www.thinkbox.tv/research/thinkbox-research/the-long-and-the-short-of-it)
- Optimal budget split: about 60% brand-building : 40% activation in *The Long and the Short of It*; 62:38 in the follow-up *Effectiveness in Context* (2018). Field stresses it varies by sector: financial services closer to 80:20, charities around 40:60. [B] (V) — [IPA blog: next chapter for Long and Short](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it/); [Campaign India interview with Peter Field](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/a-matter-of-time/418971)
- Emotional vs. rational: over the longer term emotional campaigns are "almost twice as likely" to produce top-box profit growth as rational ones; emotional campaigns reduce price sensitivity, letting brands defend or harden pricing. [B] (V, via summary) — [Alex Murrell summary of Long and Short](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)
- Earlier IPA dataBANK analysis (Pringle & Field, *Brand Immortality*, 2008): campaigns with purely emotional content performed about twice as well on profit as purely rational ones — 31% vs. 16%, mixed 26%. Note: sources describe these as profit-performance figures; whether they are "% of cases reporting very large profit gains" could not be confirmed in-session. [B/C] (V, secondary) — [Neurosciencemarketing summary](https://neurosciencemarketing.com/blog/articles/emotional-ads-work-best.htm)
- Binet (Marketing Week): brand and performance work best run as separate executions rather than one ad doing both. [C] (V) — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/les-binet-brand-performance/)
- Share-of-voice rule: brands whose share of voice exceeds share of market ("ESOV") tend to grow; IPA rule of thumb is roughly 0.5–0.6 points of annual share growth per 10 points of ESOV. [B] (NR) — [IPA deck](https://ipa.co.uk/media/14463/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final_030424.pdf)
- Peter Field, *The Crisis in Creative Effectiveness* (IPA, 2019): the efficiency advantage of creatively awarded campaigns fell from roughly 12x (c. 2008) to about 4x by 2018, attributed to short-termism and "disposable" activation-style creativity. [B] (NR) — [IPA publication page](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/publications-reports/the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness)

**Binet & Field B2B (LinkedIn B2B Institute)**
- *The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing* (2019), published by the LinkedIn-funded B2B Institute, draws on IPA B2B cases. Findings confirmed in-session: B2B needs both long-term brand building and short-term activation; emotional B2B ads outperform rational ones for brand building; customer acquisition (reach), not loyalty, is the main growth driver. [B] (V) — [LinkedIn B2B Institute report page](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/marketing-as-growth); [IPA EffWorks page](https://ipa.co.uk/effworks/marketing-marketing-v2/five-principles-of-growth-in-b2b-marketing/); [report PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf)
- The widely quoted B2B numbers — optimal split c. 46% brand : 54% activation; emotional strategies several times more effective than rational for long-term business effects; ~0.7 points share growth per 10 points ESOV — are from this report but **could not be verified in-session** (PDF host blocked). Treat as NR. [B] (NR) — [report PDF](https://news.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/news/en-us/linkedin-pressroom-page/LIN_B2B-Marketing-Report-Digital.pdf)
- Caveat: the B2B evidence base is thin; the original IPA work is "mostly B2C focused". [C] (V) — [Will Patrick notes](https://www.willpatrick.co.uk/notes/the-long-and-the-short-of-it-field-binet/)
- John Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass) "95-5 rule" (B2B Institute, 2021): only about 5% of B2B buyers are in-market in a given period, because firms change suppliers roughly every five years; Dawes himself calls it a heuristic — a category bought every two years has c. 13% in-market per quarter. Implication: advertising mostly works by building memory in future buyers. [B] (V) — [Ehrenberg-Bass: 95-5 rule](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/advertising-effectiveness-and-the-95-5-rule-most-b2b-buyers-are-not-in-the-market-right-now); [LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/it/it/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule)

**Orlando Wood / System1 (*Lemon* 2019, *Look Out* 2021, IPA)**
- *Lemon* applies Iain McGilchrist's hemisphere theory to 30+ years of TV ads and argues "right-brain" features have declined while "left-brain" features rose, and that this tracks the decline in effectiveness in the IPA Databank. [B/C] (V) — [IPA Lemon page](https://ipa.co.uk/lemon); [Contagious](https://contagious.com/news-and-views/why-left-brain-thinking-is-killing-advertising-effectiveness); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/ipa-publishes-lemon-a-repair-manual-to-reverse-the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness)
- Right-brain features (associated with stronger emotional response and long-term effects): characters with agency, dialogue, implicit/unspoken communication, a clear sense of place, a scene unfolding with progression, distinctive accents, references to culture/pastiche, wordplay, music with discernible melody, metaphor. Left-brain features (associated with weaker response): on-screen words obtruding, abstracted product/feature shots, abstracted body parts, flatness, voiceover/monologue, freeze-frames, rapid rhythmic edits, rhythmic soundtrack. [B/C] (V for metaphor, music, sense of place, dialogue, characters, on-screen text, abstract body parts, rhythmic edits, voiceover; rest NR) — [IPA Lemon page](https://ipa.co.uk/lemon); [WARC](https://cdn.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/many-tv-ads-ignore-best-practices-for-emotion-and-attention/44282); [System1 metrics explained](https://system1group.com/uncategorized/our-metrics-explained)
- System1 study of 100 UK and US ads: the more left-brain features, the less likely a strong Star Rating (System1's emotion-based predictor of long-term share growth). [C, vendor] (V) — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/are-ads-becoming-too-far-left-brain)
- System1 + Amazon Ads analysis of 150 ads: left-brain ads were about twice as likely to drive a "detail page view" (short-term intent), while right-brain elements produced a 1.6x uplift in brand recall — i.e. the two styles do different jobs. [C, vendor] (V) — [System1: The Timeless Importance of the Show](https://system1group.com/blog/the-timeless-importance-of-the-show)
- *Look Out* (2021) extends this to attention: right-brain "broad-beam" features hold attention; pairs with Nelson-Field/TVision attention data. [C] (NR) — [IPA speaker page: Orlando Wood](https://events.ipa.co.uk/event-resources/speakers/orlando-wood/)
- Scientific caveat: the popular left-brain/right-brain dichotomy is not supported as a personality/"mode" trait in neuroimaging (Nielsen, Zielinski, Ferguson, Lainhart & Anderson 2013, *PLoS ONE* 8(8): e71275, n=1,011). Wood's feature taxonomy can be empirically useful even if the neuroscience framing is metaphorical. [A] (NR) — [PLoS ONE](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0071275)

**Byron Sharp & Jenni Romaniuk / Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (University of South Australia)**
- Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (OUP, 2010): brands grow mainly by penetration (more buyers, mostly light buyers), not loyalty; loyalty follows the "double jeopardy" law (small brands have fewer buyers who are also slightly less loyal); growth requires **mental availability** (being easily thought of in buying situations) and **physical availability** ("easy to mind, easy to find"). [B, underpinned by A-grade papers] (V) — [LinkedIn/Ehrenberg-Bass intro to How B2B Brands Grow](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/me/business/en-us/marketing-solutions/resources/pdfs/b2b-introduction.pdf); [Ehrenberg-Bass B2B reports](https://marketingscience.info/b2b-reports/)
- Distinctiveness over differentiation: Romaniuk, Sharp & Ehrenberg (2007), "Evidence concerning the importance of perceived brand differentiation", *Australasian Marketing Journal* 15(2), 42–54 — buyers perceive their brands as only weakly differentiated; brands should be distinctive (recognisable) rather than rely on perceived meaningful difference. [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1441-3582(07)70042-3)
- Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (OUP, 2018): assets (colours, logos, shapes, characters, sounds, taglines) are scored on **fame** (% linking asset to brand) and **uniqueness** (% linking it only to that brand); goal is the top-right "use or lose" quadrant. [B] (NR) — [Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://www.marketingscience.info/)
- Category Entry Points (CEPs): the cues buyers use to retrieve brands in a buying situation; brands should link themselves to many CEPs before buyers come to market. [B] (V) — [Romaniuk, CEPs in B2B (LinkedIn B2B Institute PDF)](https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/lem/business/en/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2bi-cepinb2b-final.pdf)
- *How B2B Brands Grow* (Romaniuk, Sharp, Dawes & Faghidno, Nov 2023) extends the laws to B2B; a later study argues physical availability is B2B's biggest growth opportunity. [B] (V) — [Ehrenberg-Bass](https://marketingscience.info/b2b-reports/); [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/b2b-growth-physical-availability/)
- Sharp on Apple: *How Brands Grow* argues Apple's customer loyalty is roughly what double jeopardy predicts for a brand of its share, i.e. Apple is not a law-breaking "love brand". This is Sharp's reading of panel data from the 2000s. [B] (NR — not re-checked; exact figures not quoted here) — [Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://www.marketingscience.info/)

### Inferences
- The robust, cross-school consensus a small company can rely on: (1) reach future buyers, not just in-market ones; (2) be distinctive and consistent (same assets for years); (3) emotion/story/entertainment mainly earns attention and memory and reduces price sensitivity; (4) creative quality matters more than media tweaks; (5) separate "build memory" work from "convert now" work.
- The specific numbers (60/40, 46/54, 12x, 47%) are directional industry heuristics from non-random samples. The peer-reviewed evidence agrees on direction for price sensitivity and creative, but suggests average advertising effects are small and hard to measure — an argument for patience, consistency and cheap reach rather than for big spend.
- Apple fits the playbook unusually cleanly: decades of consistent distinctive assets (logo, white space, product-as-hero, typography, "Shot on iPhone"), broad reach media, and mostly emotional/demonstrative rather than spec-led ads. That Apple "proves" the playbook is, however, an n=1 inference confounded by product quality, retail and ecosystem.
- For a small B2B-ish company: the 95-5 logic and CEPs are the most transferable — most prospects are not buying now, so the job is to be remembered for specific buying situations.

### Gaps
- Exact B2B figures (46/54 split, emotional multiplier, ESOV coefficient) could not be verified because news.linkedin.com / ipa.co.uk were unreachable; verify in the report PDF before quoting.
- Exact Long-and-Short percentages for "very large profit effects" (emotional vs. rational by duration) not retrieved.
- System1's claimed mapping of Star Rating to market-share gain (e.g. 5-star ≈ up to c. 3 points of share growth) was not verified, and I found no independent academic validation of System1's Star Rating.
- The Kantar "4x profit" figure not confirmed.
- No peer-reviewed test of the 60/40 ratio exists as far as I could find; the critique that it rests on award-entry data stands unrefuted in the academic literature.

## 2. Which psychological mechanisms are relevant, and how solid is each?

### Takeaway
Most mechanisms invoked to explain Apple-style advertising are real and well-replicated in their basic form (mere exposure, fluency, affect heuristic, peak-end, narrative transportation, endowment, distinctiveness, extended self, brand attachment), but a few popular ones are weak or contested — behavioural/brand priming, Zak's oxytocin-storytelling account, hemisphere metaphors and consumer-neuroscience "love" claims. Use the robust ones as design principles and treat the contested ones as stories, not science.

### Cited Findings

**Dual-process, affect and memory**
- Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011): System 1 (fast, associative, affective) drives most everyday judgement; System 2 (slow, effortful) often merely endorses. Caveat: the book's priming chapter relies on studies that later failed to replicate; Kahneman himself acknowledged in 2017 that he "placed too much faith in underpowered studies". [A/B; ch. 4 = D] (NR) — [Schimmack et al., "Reconstruction of a Train Wreck" with Kahneman's reply](https://replicationindex.com/2017/02/02/reconstruction-of-a-train-wreck-how-priming-research-went-off-the-rails/)
- Affect heuristic: Slovic, Finucane, Peters & MacGregor (2007), "The affect heuristic", *European Journal of Operational Research* 177(3), 1333–1352 — people judge risks and benefits by the feeling an object evokes; liked things are judged high-benefit/low-risk. [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2005.04.006)
- Mere exposure: Zajonc (1968), "Attitudinal effects of mere exposure", *JPSP* 9(2, Pt.2), 1–27; meta-analyses Bornstein (1989), *Psychological Bulletin* 106(2), 265–289 and Montoya, Horton, Vevea, Citkowicz & Lauber (2017), *Psychological Bulletin* 143(5), 459–498 — robust effect, but inverted-U (liking rises then falls with heavy repetition) and stronger for brief/unattended exposures. [A] (NR) — [Zajonc DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0025848); [Bornstein DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.106.2.265); [Montoya et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000085)
- Processing fluency: Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004), "Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure: Is Beauty in the Perceiver's Processing Experience?", *Personality and Social Psychology Review* 8(4), 364–382 — the easier a stimulus is to process (symmetry, contrast, clarity, repetition), the more it is liked and judged true. Review: Alter & Oppenheimer (2009), *PSPR* 13(3), 219–235. [A] (NR) — [Reber et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0804_3); [Alter & Oppenheimer DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868309341564)
- Peak-end rule: Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993), "When More Pain Is Preferred to Less: Adding a Better End", *Psychological Science* 4(6), 401–405. Applied to ads: Baumgartner, Sujan & Padgett (1997), "Patterns of Affective Reactions to Advertisements", *JMR* 34(2), 219–232 — ad liking is driven by the emotional peak and the final moment, not the average. [A] (NR) — [Kahneman et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x); [Baumgartner et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379703400203)
- Von Restorff / isolation effect: items that differ from their context are better remembered; see Hunt (1995), "The subtlety of distinctiveness: What von Restorff really did", *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 2(1), 105–112. [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03214414)

**Story and "neuro-storytelling"**
- Narrative transportation: Green & Brock (2000), "The role of transportation in the persuasiveness of public narratives", *JPSP* 79(5), 701–721 — absorption in a story reduces counter-arguing and shifts beliefs. Meta-analysis: Van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014), "The Extended Transportation-Imagery Model", *Journal of Consumer Research* 40(5), 797–817 (76 articles) — transportation reliably increases story-consistent affect, beliefs, attitudes, intentions. [A] (NR) — [Green & Brock DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.5.701); [Van Laer et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/673383)
- Paul Zak (Claremont Graduate University): claims character-driven stories with a dramatic arc raise oxytocin, which predicts donations and prosocial behaviour; summarised in Zak (2015), "Why Inspiring Stories Make Us React: The Neuroscience of Narrative", *Cerebrum*. Related: Lin, Grewal, Morin, Johnson & Zak (2013), "Oxytocin Increases the Influence of Public Service Advertisements", *PLoS ONE* 8(2): e56934. [A-venue but contested → D as a mechanism claim] (V for Cerebrum) — [Zak 2015, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4445577); [Lin et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0056934)
- Critique: Nave, Camerer & McCullough (2015), "Does Oxytocin Increase Trust in Humans? A Critical Review of Research", *Perspectives on Psychological Science* 10(6), 772–789 — the cumulative evidence does **not** robustly show that oxytocin causes or tracks human trust; several key findings failed replication, and plasma-oxytocin assay methods disagree wildly. A later registered replication (Declerck, Boone, Pauwels, Vogt & Fehr 2020, *Nature Human Behaviour*) also failed to reproduce the oxytocin-trust effect (details not verified in-session). Zak's reply is that critiques concern supraphysiological intranasal doses, not endogenous release. [A] (V) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26581735/); [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/rj5sw-bsb59); [full text PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~memccullough/Papers/NaveCamererMcCullough15.pdf); [Zak profile incl. criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_J._Zak)

**Identity, self and attachment**

- Balanced academic review: Plassmann, Ramsøy & Milosavljevic (2012), "Branding the brain: A critical review and outlook", *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 22(1), 18–36. ### Inferences
- Mapping to Apple's advertising and product experience (my synthesis, not a tested causal claim): simplicity/white space/one idea per ad → processing fluency; decades of consistent assets and heavy reach → mere exposure + mental availability; product-as-hero in an uncluttered frame → Von Restorff; "Think Different", "Shot on iPhone", "Underdogs" → identity signalling and narrative transportation; keynote reveal + unboxing + store → peak-end and ritual; premium production values and price → costly signalling and price-quality placebo; ownership of a multi-device ecosystem → endowment + switching costs + extended self.
- The best-supported mechanisms for a small company are the cheap ones: fluency (simplicity, clarity), distinctiveness, consistency over time, a real story with characters, a designed "peak" and "end" in the customer experience, and social proof.
- Attachment/love constructs are measured by self-report and are correlational; they predict loyalty and price tolerance but do not prove that advertising causes attachment — product experience and usage frequency are plausibly the larger causes.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that causally attributes Apple's brand attachment to its advertising specifically (as opposed to product, design, retail and ecosystem).
- Declerck et al. (2020) registered replication on oxytocin and trust was identified only in a reference list; DOI and result details not verified in-session.
- Zak's "Immersion" neuro-measure is commercial and I found no independent validation.

## 3. What studies exist specifically on Apple?

### Takeaway
There is a small but real peer-reviewed literature on Apple: an (unreplicated) priming experiment, three influential qualitative/ethnographic studies of Mac/Newton devotion as quasi-religion and community, and some survey/psychophysiology work on iPhone identity. The famous brain-scan claims (BBC 2011, Lindstrom 2011) are not science: one is an n=1 TV demonstration, the other was publicly rebutted by 45 neuroscientists. Lock-in is better documented by regulators than by academics; decoy pricing and unboxing claims about Apple are plausible inferences from general research, not Apple-specific findings.

### Cited Findings

**Peer-reviewed, Apple-specific**
- Fitzsimons, Chartrand & Fitzsimons (2008), "Automatic Effects of Brand Exposure on Motivated Behavior: How Apple Makes You 'Think Different'", *Journal of Consumer Research* 35(1), 21–35 (Duke / Waterloo). 341 students subliminally exposed to the Apple vs. IBM logo; Apple-primed participants generated more — and more unusual — uses for a brick (a creativity task). A companion study used Disney vs. E! (honesty). The authors called it the first clear evidence that subliminal brand exposure changes specific behaviour. [A venue; evidential weight **contested**] (V) — [paper PDF (Duke)](https://people.duke.edu/~tlc10/bio/TLC_articles/2008/Fitzsimons_Chartrand_Fitzsimons_2008.pdf); [Duke Today](https://today.duke.edu/2008/03/apple_ibm.html); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/03/080328085918.htm); [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v35y2008i1p21-35.html)
- Replication status: I found **no published direct replication** (successful or failed) of the Apple-logo study. It belongs to the behavioural/goal-priming family that has fared badly in replication — e.g. Doyen, Klein, Pichon & Cleeremans (2012), *PLoS ONE* 7(1): e29081 (failure to replicate Bargh's elderly-priming); Open Science Collaboration (2015), *Science* 349(6251) (only c. 36–39% of psychology findings replicated; social priming especially weak). Treat as suggestive, not established. [A] (NR) — [Doyen et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0029081); [OSC DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aac4716)
- Muniz & O'Guinn (2001), "Brand Community", *JCR* 27(4), 412–432 — Macintosh users (with Saab and Bronco owners) as the founding case for the brand-community concept: consciousness of kind (incl. "oppositional loyalty" against PC/Microsoft), rituals and traditions (sharing brand history and stories), moral responsibility (helping other users). [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/319618)
- Belk & Tumbat (2005), "The Cult of Macintosh", *Consumption Markets & Culture* 8(3), 205–217 — video-ethnography; Mac devotion is sustained by four myths: a creation myth (the garage), a messianic myth (Steve Jobs), a satanic myth (IBM/Microsoft) and a resurrection myth (Jobs' return). [A, qualitative] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10253860500160403)
- Muniz & Schau (2005), "Religiosity in the Abandoned Apple Newton Brand Community", *JCR* 31(4), 737–747 — after Apple discontinued the Newton (1998), users kept the brand alive with supernatural/religious narratives: persecution, faith rewarded, survival, miraculous recovery, resurrection. Shows community can persist without (and against) the company. [A, qualitative] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/426607)
- Clayton, Leshner & Almond (2015), "The Extended iSelf: The Impact of iPhone Separation on Cognition, Emotion, and Physiology", *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* 20(2), 119–135 — iPhone users unable to answer their ringing phone showed higher heart rate/blood pressure, anxiety and worse puzzle performance; interpreted via Belk's extended self. (Small sample; about smartphones generally more than Apple.) [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcc4.12109)
- Shaw, Ellis, Kendrick, Ziegler & Wiseman (2016), "Predicting Smartphone Operating System from Personality and Individual Differences", *Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking* 19(12), 727–732 — iPhone owners were more likely to see their phone as a status object and scored higher on emotionality; small effects, UK convenience samples. [A, modest] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2016.0324)
- Bertrand & Kamenica (NBER Working Paper 24771, 2018; later *Review of Economic Studies*), "Coming Apart? Cultural Distances in the United States over Time" — in 2016, owning an iPhone was the single best brand predictor of being in the top income quartile (c. 69% accuracy); iPad second. Evidence that Apple functions as a status/identity marker in the US. [A] (NR) — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24771)

**Brain-scan claims — weak**
- BBC Three, *Secrets of the Superbrands* (2011, presenter Alex Riley): a team of neuroscientists MRI-scanned one Apple devotee (Alex Brooks, editor of World of Apple) and reported that Apple imagery activated the same areas as religious imagery does in believers. Single subject, TV production, never published in a journal. [D] (NR — bbc.com not fetchable in-session) — [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-13416598)
- Martin Lindstrom, "You Love Your iPhone. Literally.", *New York Times* op-ed, 1 Oct 2011: claimed an fMRI study of 16 subjects showed insular-cortex activation to a ringing iPhone, meaning people "love" their iPhones. [D] (V via rebuttal coverage) — [NYT op-ed](https://www.nytimes.com/2011/10/01/opinion/you-love-your-iphone-literally.html)
- Rebuttal: letter to the NYT by Russell Poldrack (then UT Austin, now Stanford) and 44 other neuroscientists from seven countries — the insula is active in roughly one third of all imaging studies, so its activation cannot indicate love (invalid reverse inference); Poldrack added that the classic love studies (Helen Fisher et al.) show reward-system rather than insula activation. Lindstrom's work is unpublished and not peer-reviewed. [A-grade expertise, published as letter/blog] (V) — [Poldrack: signers of the letter](https://poldrack.github.io/blog/posts/signers-of-letter-to-editor-of-new-york/index.html); [Poldrack: uncut letter](https://poldrack.github.io/blog/posts/nyt-letter-to-editor-uncut-version/index.html); [Research Live: "Lindstrom brain science claims 'unsupported'"](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/lindstrom-brain-science-claims-unsupported/id/4006159); [Tal Yarkoni: "the New York Times blows it big time on brain imaging"](https://talyarkoni.org/blog/tag/iphone/); [Psychology Today](https://psychologytoday.com/ie/blog/the-decision-tree/201110/do-you-really-love-your-iphone-way)

**Ecosystem lock-in, switching costs, endowment**
- General theory: Burnham, Frels & Mahajan (2003), "Consumer Switching Costs: A Typology, Antecedents, and Consequences", *Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science* 31(2), 109–126 — procedural, financial and relational switching costs each raise retention independent of satisfaction. Endowment: Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1990). [A] (NR) — [Burnham et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0092070302250897); [KKT DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/261737)
- Regulatory evidence: US Department of Justice v. Apple (filed 21 March 2024) alleges Apple maintains smartphone monopoly power by raising switching costs — degrading cross-platform messaging ("green bubbles"), limiting third-party smartwatch and wallet interoperability, restricting super-apps and cloud gaming. These are allegations, not findings. [B, legal complaint] (NR) — [DOJ press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-sues-apple-monopolizing-smartphone-markets)
- Behavioural outcome: CIRP reports iPhone loyalty of 87% in Q1 2026 (see section 4). [B] (V) — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/07/17/iphone-loyalty-rate-climbs-to-87)

**Pricing: anchoring, decoy, compromise**
- General research: Tversky & Kahneman (1974), "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases", *Science* 185(4157), 1124–1131 (anchoring); Huber, Payne & Puto (1982), "Adding Asymmetrically Dominated Alternatives", *JCR* 9(1), 90–98 (decoy/attraction effect); Simonson (1989), "Choice Based on Reasons: The Case of Attraction and Compromise Effects", *JCR* 16(2), 158–174. [A] (NR) — [T&K DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.185.4157.1124); [Huber et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/208899); [Simonson DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/209205)
- Caution: Frederick, Lee & Baskin (2014), "The Limits of Attraction", *JMR* 51(4), 487–507 — the decoy effect largely disappears with realistic, perceptually presented options (rather than numeric attribute tables); its real-world reach is narrower than textbooks imply. [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.12.0061)

**Packaging / unboxing**
- Reimann, Zaichkowsky, Neuhaus, Bender & Weber (2010), "Aesthetic package design: A behavioral, neural, and psychological investigation", *Journal of Consumer Psychology* 20(4), 431–441 — aesthetic packaging is chosen over well-known brands in standard packaging even at higher prices, with longer reaction times and reward-area activation. [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2010.06.009)
- Vohs, Wang, Gino & Norton (2013), "Rituals Enhance Consumption" — ritualised steps before consumption raise enjoyment and willingness to pay (theoretical basis for the unboxing effect). [A] (NR) — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613478949)

### Inferences
- The credible Apple-specific academic story is sociological (community, myth, identity, status marker), not neurological. "Apple lights up the God spot" should not appear in a serious playbook except as a cautionary example of neuromarketing hype.
- Muniz & Schau's Newton study implies community is built by users around shared stories, an enemy and mutual help — a company can enable this (forums, rituals, founder story, a clear "what we are against") but cannot simply advertise it into being.
- Apple's good/better/best line-ups (storage tiers, Pro vs. non-Pro, an expensive halo model) are consistent with anchoring/compromise logic, but calling them "decoys" is practitioner inference; given Frederick et al. (2014), the anchoring/halo and trade-up mechanism is better supported than a pure decoy effect.
- The Fitzsimons finding is best used as "brands carry goal-relevant associations" (well supported generally) rather than "seeing the Apple logo makes you more creative" (unreplicated).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed empirical study found on decoy/anchoring effects in Apple's actual price list.
- No peer-reviewed study found on Apple's unboxing/packaging specifically; evidence is journalistic (e.g. accounts of Apple's packaging design lab) plus the general studies above.
- No direct replication of Fitzsimons et al. (2008) located — absence of evidence, not evidence of failure.
- The BBC article could not be opened in-session; the n=1 description is from memory of the programme coverage and should be re-checked.
- Peer-reviewed quantification of Apple ecosystem lock-in (e.g. effect of owning N Apple devices on iPhone retention) not found; CIRP and court filings are the main sources.

## 4. How does emotional advertising translate into sales and pricing power?

### Takeaway
The measurable commercial signature of a strong brand is not higher ad-response but lower price sensitivity, higher retention and a disproportionate share of category revenue: Apple holds about 20% of smartphone units but roughly 43–49% of smartphone revenue, an 87% US repurchase rate, and a brand value of $0.5–1.4 trillion depending on the valuer. The causal share attributable to advertising (versus product and ecosystem) is not identified by any source.

### Cited Findings
- Price sensitivity mechanism: non-price brand advertising reduces price sensitivity (Kaul & Wittink 1995; Mela, Gupta & Lehmann 1997); IPA data: emotional campaigns "excel at reducing price sensitivity". [A / B] (NR / V) — [Kaul & Wittink DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.14.3.G151); [Mela et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379703400205); [Alex Murrell summary of Binet & Field](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)
- Attachment → willingness to pay a premium (Thomson, MacInnis & Park 2005; Park et al. 2010). [A] (NR) — [Thomson et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327663jcp1501_10); [Park et al. DOI](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.74.6.1)
- Units vs. revenue (Counterpoint): Apple had 20% of global smartphone shipments in 2025 (Samsung 19%, Xiaomi 13%), shipments +10% YoY. In Q2 2025 Apple had 17% of units but 43% of global smartphone revenue (Samsung 20% units, 15% revenue). In Q2 2026 Apple reportedly reached a record 49% of global smartphone revenue with an ASP of $946 (vs. $879 a year earlier). [B, via secondary reporting] (V) — [MacRumors, 2025 market](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/01/12/apple-tops-2025-smartphone-market/); [Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/de/insights/global-smartphone-shipments-grew-2-percent-YoY-in-2025); [9to5Mac, Q2 2026 revenue share](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/31/report-apple-hit-a-record-49-share-of-global-smartphone-revenue-for-q2/)
- Loyalty (CIRP, US): 87% of iPhone buyers in the March-2026 quarter were previous iPhone owners (84% in Q1 2025, 85% in Q1 2024); 12% of iPhone buyers switched from Android (14% a year earlier); Android switchers have ranged 11–15% in recent years. [B] (V) — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/07/17/iphone-loyalty-rate-climbs-to-87); [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/iphone-loyalty-rises-in-2026_id181884)
- A SellCell survey reports iPhone loyalty 96.4% vs. Android 86.4% in 2026 — different method (stated intent, trade-in site audience); lower reliability. [C] (V) — [TechSpot](https://techspot.com/news/113164-iphone-owners-hit-87-loyalty-rate-year-android.html)
- Kantar BrandZ 2026 (May 2026): Google is #1 at about $1.48tn (+57%); Apple #2 at about $1.38tn (+6%) after four consecutive years at #1 (2025: $1.29tn); Microsoft $1.11tn, Amazon $1.02tn, NVIDIA $815bn; Top-100 total a record $13.1tn (+22%). [B] (V) — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/brands/most-valuable-global-brands-2026); [MacTech](https://www.mactech.com/2026/05/14/google-overtakes-apple-in-the-2026-kantar-brandz-top-100-most-valuable-global-brands-rankings/amp/); [MacRumors on 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/05/16/apple-again-named-most-valuable-brand/)
- Interbrand Best Global Brands 2026 (Oct 2026): Apple #1 at $514.6bn (+9%); Microsoft $443.7bn (+14%); Google $389.3bn (+23%); NVIDIA fastest riser (+71%); top-100 total c. $3.9tn (+8.1%). 2025: Apple #1 for the 12th-plus consecutive year at $470.9bn (−4%). [B, via secondary press] (V) — [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/marketing/apple-lidera-e-nvidia-avanca-entre-marcas-mais-valiosas); [Bloomberg Línea](https://www.bloomberglinea.com/negocios/las-marcas-mas-valiosas-del-mundo-en-2026-apple-lidera-y-nvidia-registra-el-mayor-salto/); [MacRumors on 2025](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/10/16/apple-remains-worlds-most-valuable-brand-2025/)
- Brand Finance Global 500 2026 (Jan 2026): Apple remained the world's most valuable brand. [B/C] (V, value not retrieved) — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/01/21/apple-still-the-worlds-most-valuable-brand-in-2026-according-to-updated-ranking)
- Academic caution on ad-to-sales translation: median TV ad elasticity about 0.01 across 288 brands (Shapiro, Hitsch & Tuchman 2021); meta-analytic short-term 0.12 / long-term 0.24 (Sethuraman et al. 2011). ### Inferences
- The three brand-valuation rankings differ by almost a factor of three for Apple ($0.51tn Interbrand vs. $1.38tn Kantar) — these are model outputs, useful for rank order and direction, not as facts about worth.
- The units-vs-revenue gap (c. 20% of units, c. 45–49% of revenue) is the cleanest public indicator of Apple's pricing power; it is far larger than anything advertising elasticities alone would explain, which points to the combined effect of product, ecosystem lock-in and brand.
- For a small company the translation chain to target is: distinctive, emotional, consistent communication → mental availability and liking → less price comparison/negotiation and higher retention. Measure it with price realisation (discount depth, win rate at list price), retention and branded search — not with last-click ROAS.
- Apple's 87% loyalty must be read alongside Sharp's double-jeopardy point and the switching-cost evidence: high retention is partly structural (duopoly, ecosystem), not purely emotional.

### Gaps
- No public estimate of Apple's own price elasticity of demand from a peer-reviewed source was found.
- Smartphone **profit** share figures for 2025/2026 not found (only revenue share).
- No source isolates advertising's causal contribution to Apple's premium.
- Interbrand 2026 figures come from secondary (Portuguese/Spanish-language) coverage, not Interbrand's own release.

## 5. Fact-check: "Apple is the largest company in the world" (Nov 2026) and Apple's advertising spend

### Takeaway
As of mid-November 2026 the premise is approximately right by market capitalisation — Apple retook the #1 spot from Nvidia in late July 2026, became the second-ever $5tn company, and trackers show it #1 in October/November 2026 — but the lead over Nvidia has flipped back and forth and the exact November ranking rests on low-reliability sources. Apple is not the largest company by revenue or employees. Apple has not disclosed advertising spend since FY2015 ($1.8bn); everything since is estimate.

### Cited Findings

**Market cap**

## 6. Who are the most credible people and institutions to follow or consult?

### Takeaway
Credibility splits into three tiers: peer-reviewed consumer-psychology academics (strongest on mechanisms, weakest on "what should I spend"), evidence-based marketing institutes and effectiveness practitioners (strongest on practical rules, with funding and sample caveats), and popularisers/neuromarketers (engaging, but treat as hypotheses). For a small company, the most directly usable are Ehrenberg-Bass (Romaniuk, Dawes), Binet & Field, Orlando Wood and Mark Ritson as synthesiser.

### Cited Findings

**Tier 1 — academics (mechanisms; high reliability)**
- C. Whan Park and Deborah J. MacInnis (USC Marshall) — brand attachment: Thomson, MacInnis & Park 2005; Park et al. 2010. — [JM paper](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkg.74.6.1)
- Rajeev Batra, Richard Bagozzi (Michigan Ross) and Aaron Ahuvia (Michigan-Dearborn) — brand love. — [JM paper](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.09.0339)
- Russell Belk (York University, Schulich) — extended self; "The Cult of Macintosh". — [JCR 1988](https://doi.org/10.1086/209154); [CMC 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/10253860500160403)
- Albert Muniz Jr. (DePaul), Thomas O'Guinn (Wisconsin), Hope Jensen Schau (UC Irvine, formerly Arizona) — brand community, Apple Newton. — [JCR 2001](https://doi.org/10.1086/319618); [JCR 2005](https://doi.org/10.1086/426607)
- Gavan Fitzsimons and Tanya Chartrand (Duke Fuqua), Gráinne Fitzsimons (Duke) — non-conscious brand effects (with replication caveat). — [paper](https://people.duke.edu/~tlc10/bio/TLC_articles/2008/Fitzsimons_Chartrand_Fitzsimons_2008.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Ampeon the highest-value, lowest-cost reading list is: Romaniuk on distinctive assets and CEPs; Binet & Field's B2B report; Dawes' 95-5 paper; Wood's *Lemon* feature list as a creative checklist; Ritson for balance; and Shapiro et al. (2021) as a sanity check against over-promising ROI.
- If consulting is wanted: Ehrenberg-Bass (corporate sponsorship programme) and System1 (ad testing) are the institutional routes; the academics in Tier 1 are best approached through their published scales (e.g. Park et al.'s attachment scale, Batra et al.'s brand-love scale), which can be used in customer surveys without anyone's permission.
- Every Tier-2 source has a commercial interest in the conclusion "invest in brand advertising / creative / attention"; triangulate with Tier 1.

- Current affiliations (as of Nov 2026) were not individually re-verified; several are from knowledge up to mid-2026 and people move.
