## 1. What are the recurring structural templates in Apple ads, and which campaigns exemplify each?

### Takeaway
Across 40 years Apple recycles a small set of templates: (1) brand manifesto with no product, (2) comedy series in a fixed format, (3) music-and-movement spot where the product is a prop for a visual "magic" effect, (4) one hero, one feature, one song demo, (5) emotional short film where the product enables a human payoff, (6) long-form branded-entertainment comedy, (7) metaphor/threat film for an intangible benefit (privacy), (8) real-user proof (Shot on iPhone, 911, Dear Apple). The last eight years lean heavily on (4), (5), (6) and (7), with length rising to 2–10 minutes for online films.

### Cited Findings

**Template 1: Manifesto / brand film, product absent or last**
- "1984": Chiat/Day, directed by Ridley Scott, shot in London with local skinheads cast as the drones; the full 60-second version ran once in the Super Bowl on 22 Jan 1984; the product is not shown, and the closing voice-over is "On January 24th, Apple Computer will introduce the Macintosh. And you'll see why 1984 won't be like 1984." — [Wikipedia: 1984 (advertisement)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement)) [T-ish]; [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/January-22/apple-1984-commercial-airs-during-super-bowl-xviii) [T]
- "Think Different" (1997): debuted 28 Sep 1997 as a 60-second spot during the network premiere of Toy Story; $90M campaign; footage of historical figures, with the Apple logo and "Think Different" appearing only at the end; script by Rob Siltanen with Lee Clow and team; Richard Dreyfuss narration chosen over a Steve Jobs read on the morning of first airing because Jobs said it was about Apple, not himself. — [Wikipedia: Think different](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different)
- Siltanen's first-person account says Jobs was highly involved but "far from the mastermind", and credits art director Craig Tanimoto with the concept; coverage says Jobs initially hated the spot. — [Forbes (original URL as cited by Wikipedia)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/onmarketing/2011/12/14/the-real-story-behind-apples-think-different-campaign/); summarised by [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2011/12/14/behind-the-scenes-of-apples-think-different-campaign) [A] and [The Week](https://theweek.com/articles/479403/how-apple-really-came-think-different-5-takeaways) [T]

**Template 2: Comedy series in a rigid repeatable format**
- "Get a Mac" (May 2006 – Oct 2009): two personified characters (Justin Long as Mac, John Hodgman as PC) against a plain white background; all 66 aired spots directed by Phil Morrison (Epoch Films) for TBWA\Media Arts Lab; the team shot 323 spots over three years and aired 66; won the Grand Effie in 2007. — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1681517/15-things-you-never-knew-about-apple-s-get-a-mac-campaign.html) [T]; [Campaign oral history, part 2](https://campaignlive.com/article/oral-history-get-mac-part-2/1417768) [T]; [Wikipedia: Get a Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_a_Mac)
- "Relax, it's iPhone" (2023– ): Contagious describes the formula as one hero character using their iPhone to demonstrate one product feature, paired with music that lifts the idea; the music acts as the protagonist's inner monologue and there is no dialogue. — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/iq/article/relax-its-iphone) [T]
- 2026 MacBook Neo social work: absurdist TikTok ads and a livestream character the internet dubbed "Little Finder Guy", who then got his own set of videos. — [AppleInsider, 9 Mar 2026](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/03/09/apples-tiktok-ads-for-the-macbook-neo-are-the-right-kind-of-weird) [A]; [9to5Mac, 2 Apr 2026](https://9to5mac.com/2026/04/02/watch-new-macbook-neo-ads-starring-the-adorable-little-finder-guy/) [A]

**Template 3: Music + movement + one visual trick (iPod / AirPods / HomePod lineage)**
- iPod silhouettes: designed in 2003 by Chiat/Day art director Susan Alinsangan with Lee Clow and James Vincent (a former DJ); outdoor ran first in Los Angeles in Sept 2003; Apple reportedly spent $49.6M on the campaign Jan–Aug 2004. — [Wikipedia: iPod advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod_advertising)
- AirPods: Tor Myhren (Fast Company, 2026) dates the AirPods approach to "Stroll" (2017), a black-and-white film with street dancer Lil Buck that paid homage to Apple's music-marketing DNA, and sums the formula up as "music-plus-magic-plus-dance-equals-AirPods". — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91512780/apple-vp-of-marketing-tor-myhren-10-years) [T] (preview text only)
- "Bounce" (AirPods, 2019): two-minute black-and-white film directed by Oscar Hudson, choreographed by Yoann Bourgeois, music Tessellated "I Learnt Some Jazz Today"; built practically with trampolines embedded in a raised city set. — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/oscar-hudson-defies-gravity-in-beautifully-bouncy-apple-film) [T]; [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/awards/professional/2020/231981/airpods-bounce/) [T]
- "Welcome Home" (HomePod, 2018): four-minute film by Spike Jonze with FKA twigs dancing to an Anderson .Paak track; practical moving set, "very little CGI". — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2018/03/22/behind-scenes-spike-jonze-homepod/) [A]; [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2018/03/07/apple-commissions-spike-jonze-film-idiosyncratic-homepod-ad) [T]
- "Someday" (AirPods 4 with ANC, March 2025): 5+ minute Spike Jonze film starring Pedro Pascal, a break-up story in which noise cancellation flips a grey world into a colourful dance number. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/03/18/apple-debuts-spike-jonze-film-airpods-4/) [A]; [Campaign, Ad of the Week](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/1910919/ad-week-apple-airpods-someday) [T]

**Template 4: Single-feature demo (one hero, one feature, one track)**
- "Relax, it's iPhone" spots each isolate one feature (Crash Detection, battery life via a farmer hauling a giant pumpkin on a tractor, Action Mode). — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/07/10/apple-launches-relax-iphone-14-ads-about-battery-life-and-crash-detection) [A]; [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2023/07/11/ad-the-day-apple-spot-shows-farmer-transporting-huge-pumpkin-down-lonely-road) [T]
- Apple Watch fall detection was sold in a 15-second ad showing the watch auto-dialling 911 after a hard fall. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/01/01/apple-watch-emergency-sos-911/) [A]

**Template 5: Emotional short film, product as enabler (holiday films, accessibility)**
- "Misunderstood" (2013): 90-second spot; a teen who seems glued to his phone turns out to have been filming the family to cut a gift video. — [TIME](https://time.com/archive/7065592/apples-latest-holiday-ad-will-tug-at-your-heartstrings/) [T]. - "Fuzzy Feelings" (2023): almost four minutes, stop-motion by Anna Mantzaris, shot on iPhone. — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/11/21/apples-new-stop-motion-fuzzy-feelings-holiday-ad-was-shot-on-iphone) [A]
- "Heartstrings" (2024): TBWA\Media Arts Lab, directed by Henry-Alex Rubin (Smuggler), sound design by three-time Oscar winner Paul N. J. Ottosson; a father with hearing loss uses the AirPods Pro 2 hearing-aid feature to hear his daughter play "Our House" on Christmas morning. — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/apple-highlights-transformative-power-of-airpods-in-2024-holiday-ad) [T]; [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2024/11/28/ad-the-day-apple-touts-hearing-aid-feature-touching-father-daughter-tale) [T]
- "A Critter Carol" (2025): 2 min 20 s puppet film directed by Mark Molloy, a cappella soundtrack, featuring iPhone 17 Pro. — [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/apple-s-holiday-film-stars-a-bunch-of-whimsical-puppet-critters-and-an-iphone-17-pro) [T]
- "The Dreamer" (12 Nov 2026): 4-minute holiday short with TBWA\Media Arts Lab, directed by Melina Matsoukas, artwork by Hebru Brantley; a young man rediscovers his creativity after his parents give him a MacBook Neo; first holiday campaign under CEO John Ternus. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/apples-heartfelt-holiday-ad-gifts-the-power-of-belief/) [T]; [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/11/12/apple-2026-holiday-ad) [A]

- "The Whole Working-From-Home Thing" (2020) ran almost 7 minutes; "Escape from the Office" (2022) ran 8–9 minutes; "OOO (Out of Office)" (2024) ran about 10 minutes. — [Cannes Lions entry via Love the Work](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/the-whole-working-from-home-thing-715591) [S]; [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/781190/apples-clever-escape-from-the-office-ad-hits-youtube-top-10/) [A]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/08/02/apple-pulls-latest-underdogs-ad) [A]

**Template 7: Metaphor / threat film for an intangible benefit (privacy)**
- "Data Auction" (May 2022): 90-second spot; a young woman, Ellie, walks into a Sotheby's-style auction where her personal data is being sold; end tag "It's your data. iPhone helps keep it that way."; OOH line "Privacy. That's iPhone." — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90753303/apple-privacy-ad-may-2022) [T]; [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/apple-holds-an-auction-for-your-data-in-privacy-policy-push) [T]
- "Flock" (July 2024): two-minute film directed by Ivan Zacharias, TBWA\Media Arts Lab; surveillance cameras behave like birds stalking phone users, in a Hitchcock "The Birds" register; campaign line "A browser that's actually private". — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/07/16/hitchcock-style-flying-camera-ad-promotes-safaris-privacy-protections) [A]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/07/16/apple-safari-ad-campaign/) [A]; [Forbes CZ](https://forbes.cz/apple-vypustil-spot-o-soukromi-na-internetu-natocil-ho-cesky-reziser-ivan-zacharias/) [T]

**Template 8: Real-user proof / documentary evidence**
- "Shot on iPhone" began in 2014–15; the "World Gallery" used photos from 162 iPhone 6 users on billboards, ran in 75 cities in 25 countries with 10,000+ installations, won the 2015 Cannes Outdoor Grand Prix, and Myhren called it "a ridiculously simple idea" based on people already posting and hashtagging their photos. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2015/06/23/apple-wins-grand-prix-shot-on/) [A]; [9to5Mac on the Adweek interview](https://9to5mac.com/2021/11/11/apple-exec-explains-the-creation-of-its-shot-on-iphone-campaign/) [A]
- Apple Watch "911" (1 Jan 2022): 60 seconds built on real 911 call audio from three people (Amanda, Jason, Jim); Apple confirmed to Gizmodo the calls were real. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/01/01/apple-watch-life-saving-911-ad/) [A]; [Contagious](https://contagious.com/news-and-views/campaing-of-the-week-911-call-ad-frames-apple-watch-as-life-saver) [T]
- "Dear Apple": built on emails Apple receives from Watch customers alerted to health conditions or helped by Emergency SOS / Crash Detection; a version was shown at the Sept 2025 event. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/09/13/dear-apple/) [A]
- Sept 2026: a "Shot on iPhone" piece used ROSÉ's music video filmed on iPhone 18 Pro. — [MacRumors Apple Ads roundup](https://macrumors.com/roundup/apple-ads) [A] (search extract only)

**Template 9: Cultural-reference montage for a new category**
- Vision Pro "Get Ready" (Jan 2024): montage of film clips of characters putting on goggles, helmets and headsets, opening with Gene Wilder in Young Frankenstein shouting "Get ready!"; ends with a woman putting on Vision Pro at home; an explicit callback to the 2007 iPhone "Hello" teaser shown at the Oscars. — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2024/01/08/apple-vision-pro-get-ready-ad) [A]; [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/01/08/vision-pro-ad-movie-clips/) [A]

### Inferences
- Analyst interpretation: the templates map onto product maturity. New categories get a cultural-reference montage or a manifesto (no feature claims), mature products get single-feature demos, and intangible benefits (privacy, accessibility, health) get either a metaphor film or real-user evidence.
- The comedy-series template has returned repeatedly (Get a Mac, Underdogs, Relax, Little Finder Guy), and it is the template most associated with both Apple's biggest effectiveness award (Grand Effie) and several of its pulled ads (Genius, OOO, The Parent Presentation). Humour is high-variance for Apple.
- In templates 3 and 5 the product is rarely the protagonist; it is the trigger for a transformation (grey to colour, silence to hearing, isolation to connection).

- "Behind the Mac" (2018 onward): no usable source surfaced in this session. [background, not verified this session] It is a mostly black-and-white campaign of people photographed/filmed from behind their open MacBooks, with a launch film set to Daniel Johnston's "Story of an Artist".
- "The Surprise" (2019 holiday) and "Privacy. That's iPhone / Private Side" length and credits were not confirmed.
- Apple Intelligence and Vision Pro post-launch ads beyond those listed were not researched in depth.
- No iPhone 18 launch film details surfaced.

## 2. How do the first 1–5 seconds work, and when does the product/brand first appear?

### Takeaway
Documented evidence on opening seconds is thin because no ad could be viewed in this session; what trade coverage does document is that Apple typically opens on a person, a sound or an odd situation, not on a logo, and reserves brand/product for the end (manifesto and metaphor films) or for the turning point (enabler films).

### Cited Findings
- Sound as hook: "911" opens straight into real emergency-call audio from people in danger, laid over calm imagery, with the three stories intertwined over 60 seconds. — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/an-apple-watch-can-save-your-life-in-an-emergency-demonstrates-tense-ad) [T]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/01/01/apple-watch-life-saving-911-ad/) [A]
- A shouted line as hook: Vision Pro "Get Ready" begins with Gene Wilder exclaiming "Get Ready!". — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2024/01/08/apple-vision-pro-get-ready-ad) [A]
- Brand last: in "Think Different" the Apple logo appeared only at the very end with the tagline. — [Wikipedia: Think different](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different)
- Brand last: "1984" names the product only in the closing voice-over and never shows it. — [Wikipedia: 1984 (advertisement)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement))
- Product at the end as resolution: "Get Ready" withholds the Vision Pro until the final shot of a woman putting it on. — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2024/01/08/apple-vision-pro-get-ready-ad) [A]
- No dialogue, music carries the story: the "Relax, it's iPhone" spots have no dialogue and use the track as inner monologue. — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/iq/article/relax-its-iphone) [T]
- Intrigue through a human in an odd place: "Data Auction" follows Ellie stumbling into an auction of her own data. — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90753303/apple-privacy-ad-may-2022) [T]

### Inferences
- Analyst interpretation: Apple's hooks are situational (a person in a slightly wrong situation) or auditory (a voice, a track starting), and the brand reveal is delayed. This works for Apple because of pre-existing brand recognition and distinctive assets (white product shots, typography, end-card logo); a lesser-known brand copying the late reveal would risk low attribution. This caveat matters for any transfer to another brand.
- In the fixed-format series ("Get a Mac") the format itself (white void, two men, "Hello, I'm a Mac") functions as instant branding in the first second, so no logo is needed up front. [background, not verified this session: the "Hello, I'm a Mac / And I'm a PC" opening line.]

### Gaps
- No source gave timestamps for first product or logo appearance in any spot. A proper answer requires viewing the films on Apple's YouTube channel and logging them shot by shot.
- No published study was found that measures Apple's first-seconds attention specifically (e.g. skip rates or attention data).

## 3. Typical lengths, and how long films are cut down

### Takeaway
Apple runs a wide length ladder: 15 s feature/social cuts, 30 s and 60 s TV spots, 90 s to 2 min 30 s "films" for privacy, accessibility and AirPods, 4–5.5 min holiday and director-led shorts, and 7–10 min Apple at Work comedies. Sources document lengths well but say little about cutdown methodology.

### Cited Findings
- 15 s: Apple Watch fall-detection ad; HomePod was first sold with 15-second spots before the four-minute "Welcome Home". — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/01/01/apple-watch-emergency-sos-911/) [A]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2018/03/22/behind-scenes-spike-jonze-homepod/) [A]
- 30 s: Apple's own award entry says the Action Mode film was the top-performing 30 s video ad of "Relax, it's iPhone" to date. — [Love the Work / Cannes Lions entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/relax-its-iphone-action-mode-1524482) [S]
- 30–39 s: Vision Pro "Get Ready" is given as about 30 s by AppleInsider and 39 s by Digital Trends (conflict). — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/01/09/apple-released-a-short-video-teasing-apple-vision-pro) [A]; [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/apple-get-ready-ad-for-vision-pro-headset) [T]
- 60 s: "1984" (a 30 s cut existed but did not run in the game), "Think Different" launch spot, "911", the "Relax" tractor spot. — [Wikipedia: 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement)); [Wikipedia: Think different](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/01/01/apple-watch-life-saving-911-ad/) [A]; [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2023/07/11/ad-the-day-apple-spot-shows-farmer-transporting-huge-pumpkin-down-lonely-road) [T]
- 90 s: "Misunderstood", "Data Auction" (to run on social and YouTube). — [TIME](https://time.com/archive/7065592/apples-latest-holiday-ad-will-tug-at-your-heartstrings/) [T]; [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90753303/apple-privacy-ad-may-2022) [T]
- 2 min – 2 min 30 s: "Bounce", "Flock", "The Greatest", "A Critter Carol" (2:20). — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/oscar-hudson-defies-gravity-in-beautifully-bouncy-apple-film) [T]; [Forbes CZ](https://forbes.cz/apple-vypustil-spot-o-soukromi-na-internetu-natocil-ho-cesky-reziser-ivan-zacharias/) [T]; [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/accessibility-work-swept-awards-cannes-%e2%80%94-its-soon-celebrate/1828517) [T]; [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/apple-s-holiday-film-stars-a-bunch-of-whimsical-puppet-critters-and-an-iphone-17-pro) [T]

- 7–10 min: Underdogs films (almost 7; 8–9, sources conflict between "more than 8" and 9; about 10 for OOO). — [Love the Work](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/the-whole-working-from-home-thing-715591) [S]; [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/781190/apples-clever-escape-from-the-office-ad-hits-youtube-top-10/) [A]; [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/apple-apologizes-for-another-ad-that-missed-the-mark-220045564.html) [T]
- Rollout as a sequence of formats rather than one film: the 2026 MacBook Neo launch ran in "four acts": a birth announcement, a specs explainer and an unboxing; a live "Matcha Break" at Apple Park; then twelve videos on the colour palette. — [Famous Campaigns](https://www.famouscampaigns.com/2026/04/apple-shows-the-handmade-craft-behind-macbook-neo-launch-film/) [B, marketing blog]
- Teaser then film: "Someday" was preceded by a trailer two days before release. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/03/16/airpods-4-film-trailer/) [A]
- Making-of as a second asset: Adweek ran a roughly seven-minute behind-the-scenes film for "Welcome Home". — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/03/22/spike-jonze-homepod-ad-behind-the-scenes/) [A]

### Inferences
- Analyst interpretation: Apple treats the long film as the YouTube hero and surrounds it with a trailer, a making-of and short feature cuts, instead of only chopping the hero down. The Neo "four acts" structure suggests native-per-platform assets (livestream, TikTok absurdism, colour shorts) rather than cutdowns of one master.
- Length tracks the job: 15–30 s for one feature, 60–90 s for a metaphor or proof film, 2–5 min where an emotional arc or choreography needs room, 7–10 min only for episodic comedy with recurring characters.

### Gaps
- The "0:38" length in the brief could not be confirmed for any specific spot.
- No source described Apple's cutdown process (which scenes survive in :15/:30 versions, aspect ratios, or how 9:16 versions are produced). Apple's YouTube channel would need to be audited directly.

## 4. How is copy written, and what do Apple's creatives say about their principles?

### Takeaway
The documented principle is one idea per ad: Myhren's "nail theory" (one nail penetrates, a bed of nails does not), Segall's "simplicity", and campaign formulas that state "one hero, one feature". Taglines are short declaratives, often two beats with a full stop ("Privacy. That's iPhone."), and end lines carry the benefit while the film carries the feeling.

### Cited Findings
- Tor Myhren's "nail theory" (credited to Tracy Wong of Wongdoody): ads are like stepping on a bed of nails, nothing penetrates because there are too many; step on one nail and it makes an impact. — [Fast Company, 2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91512780/apple-vp-of-marketing-tor-myhren-10-years) [T] (preview text; wording is paraphrase)
- Myhren on Shot on iPhone: "a ridiculously simple idea ... based on behavior we were seeing with people posting their photos and hashtagging them"; "When you have a platform that's this rich, it's boundless where it can go." — [9to5Mac quoting Adweek](https://9to5mac.com/2021/11/11/apple-exec-explains-the-creation-of-its-shot-on-iphone-campaign/) [A]
- Ken Segall (ex-Apple agency creative director, named the iMac, worked on Think Different) built his book around simplicity as the single organising idea. — [Penguin Random House](https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/310710/insanely-simple-by-ken-segall) [publisher copy]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2012/04/25/insanely-simple-shares-anecdotes-on-steve-jobs-and-apple) [A]
- Segall on the 2012 Genius ads: "I honestly can't remember a single Apple campaign that's been received so poorly." — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2012/07/30/former-apple-ad-man-slams-genius-tv-spots-as-landing-with-a-thud/) [A]
- Jobs chose the Dreyfuss read for "Think Different" because the spot was about Apple, not about him; Clow had argued a Jobs voice-over would be "really powerful". — [Wikipedia: Think different](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Think_different)
- Taglines and end lines documented: "And you'll see why 1984 won't be like 1984."; "Think Different."; "Privacy. That's iPhone."; "It's your data. iPhone helps keep it that way."; "A browser that's actually private."; "Relax, it's iPhone."; "AI for the rest of us." — [Wikipedia: 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement)); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90753303/apple-privacy-ad-may-2022) [T]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/07/16/apple-safari-ad-campaign/) [A]; [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/iq/article/relax-its-iphone) [T]; [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/apple-pulls-available-now-claim-from-ai-ads-after-watchdog-probe/) [B]
- Formula stated in trade analysis: one hero character, one product feature, music as the voice, no dialogue. — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/iq/article/relax-its-iphone) [T]
- Paul Graham on "Crush!": "Steve wouldn't have shipped that ad. It would have pained him too much to watch." — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/05/10/apple-apology-ipad-crush-advert/) [T]

### Inferences
- Analyst interpretation of the copy pattern: (a) two-beat taglines with a hard stop, product name last ("Privacy. That's iPhone."); (b) imperative or reassurance in three or four words ("Relax, it's iPhone", "Get Ready", "Think Different"); (c) benefit phrased from the user's side ("It's your data"); (d) spec language pushed to supers or omitted.
- The films with the least copy (Relax spots, Bounce, Welcome Home) rely on music to do the "talking", so "short copy" is partly a consequence of music-led structure.

### Gaps
- The well-known anecdotes (Jobs/Clow tossing one versus five paper balls; "1,000 songs in your pocket" origin; Isaacson's advertising chapters; direct Lee Clow quotes) could not be sourced in this session. [background, not verified this session] The paper-ball anecdote is told in Segall's "Insanely Simple" with Lee Clow demonstrating to Jobs that people catch one idea but not five.
- The full Fast Company interview with Myhren (nail theory, AirPods formula, post-"Crush" process changes) was not readable; it is the single most valuable primary source to open for exact quotes.

## 5. How is music chosen and used?

### Takeaway
Music is a structural element, not a bed: campaigns are described as built around a track that acts as the character's voice, AirPods/iPod work is defined by music plus dance, and emotional films use a recognisable song as the payoff the product unlocks.

### Cited Findings
- Myhren's formula: "music-plus-magic-plus-dance-equals-AirPods", starting with "Stroll" (2017, Lil Buck, black and white). — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91512780/apple-vp-of-marketing-tor-myhren-10-years) [T]
- "Relax, it's iPhone": music delivers the product benefit and acts as inner monologue; "R.I.P. Leon" is set to "ALIVE" by Hanni El Khatib. — [Contagious](https://www.contagious.com/iq/article/relax-its-iphone) [T]
- "Bounce": scored with an extended version of Tessellated's "I Learnt Some Jazz Today", a little-known artist at the time. — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/oscar-hudson-defies-gravity-in-beautifully-bouncy-apple-film) [T]
- "Welcome Home": FKA twigs dances to an Anderson .Paak track. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2018/03/06/apple-homepod-speakers-commercial-spike-jonze-fka-twigs-anderson-paak/) [T]
- "Someday": two-track structure, a melancholy song ("Conticinio" by Guitarricadelafuente) followed by an upbeat one ("Perfect" by Sam i & Tropkillaz feat. Bia & MC Pikachu) when noise cancellation kicks in. — [Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/?p=893758) [A]
- "Heartstrings": the plot turns on hearing a specific song, Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young's "Our House", with Oscar-winning sound designer Paul N. J. Ottosson rendering hearing loss. — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/apple-highlights-transformative-power-of-airpods-in-2024-holiday-ad) [T]
- "The Greatest": built on Spinifex Gum's "I Am the Greatest (with Marilya Choir)"; won the Grand Prix in Entertainment Lions for Music per Campaign. — [Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/accessibility-work-swept-awards-cannes-%e2%80%94-its-soon-celebrate/1828517) [T]
- "A Critter Carol": a cappella number performed by the puppet characters. — [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/apple-s-holiday-film-stars-a-bunch-of-whimsical-puppet-critters-and-an-iphone-17-pro) [T]
- iPod silhouettes were co-created by James Vincent, a former DJ and musician. — [Wikipedia: iPod advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IPod_advertising)

### Inferences
- Analyst interpretation: three recurring uses. (1) Track as narrator replacing voice-over (Relax). (2) Track switch as the product moment: the music changes when the product is used (Someday; Heartstrings' muffled-to-clear sound). (3) Track as choreography spine (Bounce, Welcome Home, Stroll).
- Apple mixes emerging artists (Tessellated, Guitarricadelafuente) with heritage songs (CSNY); the choice follows the audience and emotion of the film rather than a single policy.

### Gaps
- "Breaking artists" effect (Feist "1234", Jet "Are You Gonna Be My Girl", Yael Naim "New Soul") could not be sourced this session. [background, not verified this session] These iPod/MacBook Air ads are widely credited with chart jumps for the artists.
- No source confirmed whether Apple edits picture to a pre-selected track ("music-first editing"); the George Harrison "Isn't It a Pity" credit for "Fuzzy Feelings" appeared only in a reader comment and is unverified.

## 6. Scene-by-scene breakdowns of well-documented spots

### Takeaway
Fourteen spots are outlined below at the level of detail trade coverage supports (beat structure, not timecodes). The common skeleton is: human situation or tension, a turn caused by the product or by a reveal, emotional or comic payoff, then a short end line and logo.

### Cited Findings

1. **"1984" (Macintosh, 60 s, 1984).** Beats: grey, uniform drones (skinheads cast in London) under a Big Brother-style screen; an athletic woman as the disruptive element; closing voice-over naming the Macintosh and the date; product never shown. Board hated it and Sculley asked Chiat/Day to sell the airtime back; the agency deliberately did not sell the 60 s slot. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_(advertisement)); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/29867/how-apples-1984-ad-was-almost-canceled) [T]. [background, not verified this session: she runs in with a sledgehammer chased by guards and hurls it into the screen, which explodes in light.]

3. **"Get a Mac" (66 aired spots, 2006–09).** Fixed stage: white void, Mac and PC side by side, a short comic exchange dramatising one PC weakness or Mac strength per spot. — [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/1681517/15-things-you-never-knew-about-apple-s-get-a-mac-campaign.html) [T]
4. **"Misunderstood" (holiday, 90 s, 2013).** Beats: family Christmas gathering; teen appears withdrawn on his iPhone; reveal that he was recording everything; he plays the edited film as his gift. — [TIME](https://time.com/archive/7065592/apples-latest-holiday-ad-will-tug-at-your-heartstrings/) [T]

15. **"The Dreamer" (holiday, 4 min, 2026).** Beats: a young man has lost touch with his creativity; his parents give him a MacBook Neo; he starts making again, with Hebru Brantley's artwork as the visual world. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/apples-heartfelt-holiday-ad-gifts-the-power-of-belief/) [T]; [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/11/12/apple-2026-holiday-ad) [A]

### Inferences
- Shared skeleton (analyst interpretation): (1) a person in a recognisable state (bored commute, heartbreak, isolation, threat); (2) a single trigger, usually the product being put on or switched on; (3) a visual or audible transformation that is the feature rendered as a feeling; (4) a quiet return; (5) end line and logo. "Crush!" inverts step 3: the transformation destroys loved objects, which is the simplest reading of why it failed.
- Reveal-based films ("Misunderstood", "911", "Data Auction") hold back one piece of information and pay it off late; that late payoff is why they need 60–90 s.

### Gaps
- Timecodes, shot counts, average shot length and exact product screen time are not documented in any source found; they need manual logging from Apple's YouTube uploads.
- Pacing and editing rhythm (cuts per second, use of single takes) could not be sourced.

## 7. Documented failures and what they teach

### Takeaway
Since 2024 Apple has pulled or apologised for an unusual number of ads; the documented causes fall into four types: a metaphor that reads as destruction ("Crush!"), cultural stereotyping ("OOO"), promising features that did not ship (Apple Intelligence Siri), and humour judged cringe or condescending ("Genius", "The Parent Presentation", early Apple Intelligence spots).

### Cited Findings
- **"Crush!" (iPad Pro, May 2024).** Myhren to Ad Age: "We missed the mark with this video, and we're sorry." Apple cancelled the planned TV run; the YouTube video stayed up. Observers noted its similarity to a 2008 LG phone spot. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/05/10/apple-apology-ipad-crush-advert/) [T]; [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/2328732/apple-apologizes-for-ipad-pro-crush-ad-and-scraps-tv-plans.html) [T]
- System1 scored "Crush!" 1.9 stars; it also tested a reversed edit (objects coming back to life), which scored 1.8. The follow-up iPad Pro ad "Worlds Made on iPad" scored 1.5, lower than "Crush!". — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/apple-crush-scores-19-stars-with-system1) [T]; [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/new-ipad-pro-ad-scores-worse-than-crush) [T]
- **"The Underdogs: OOO" (Aug 2024).** The roughly 10-minute film set in Thailand was pulled about two weeks after upload and Apple apologised after Thai citizens, influencers and lawmakers called its depiction outdated and stereotypical, with boycott calls; shot in Thailand with Indochina Productions. — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-08-02/apple-pulls-commercial-after-thai-backlash-calls-for-boycott) [T]; [TIME](https://time.com/7006872/apple-pulls-offensive-commercial-thailand-underdogs-ooo-out-of-office) [T]; [Bangkok Post](https://bangkokpost.com/life/tech/2840337/apple-apologises-and-removes-controversial-ad) [T]
- **Apple Intelligence, tone (autumn 2024).** TidBITS argued the Writing Tools and Memories ads portray users as unwilling to put in any effort; Fast Company headlined "In Apple's new Apple Intelligence ads, we're all total idiots". — [TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2024/11/11/misguided-apple-intelligence-ads/) [A]; [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91218041/apple-intelligence-ad-total-idiots) [T]
- **Apple Intelligence, "More Personal Siri" with Bella Ramsey (pulled March 2025).** The ad showed Siri recalling the name of someone met at a restaurant; Apple set it to private after confirming the feature was delayed; the other Ramsey ads, showing shipped features, stayed. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/03/07/apple-pulls-ad-that-showed-siri-being-useful-after-delaying-feature-that-could-make-siri-useful/) [A]; [Daring Fireball](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/03/08/apple-pulls-bella-ramsey-ad-that-promoted-vaporware-personalized-siri-feature) [B, expert blog]
- The National Advertising Division found Apple's ads "reasonably conveyed the message" that unreleased features were available; Apple said it would permanently discontinue the "More Personal Siri" spot; false-advertising class actions followed. — [NBC San Diego / CNBC](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/apple-ai-ads-did-not-make-adequate-disclosures-watchdog-says/3809840/) [T]; [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/apple-intelligence) [primary]; [Truth in Advertising](https://truthinadvertising.org/articles/apple-intelligence-lawsuits-mounting/) [T]
- **"The Parent Presentation" / "Convince your parents to get you a Mac" (June 2025).** Featured comedian Martin Herlihy; made private within about a day; Apple gave no reason; MacRumors notes viewers called it cringe and labels the reception explanation as speculation; coverage framed it as the fourth pulled Apple ad in about a year. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/06/21/apple-pulls-the-parent-presentation-video/) [A]; [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/apple-yanked-yet-another-ad-173036051.html) [T]; [heise](https://heise.de/-10456472) [T]
- **"Genius" ads (2012 Olympics).** Agency said they were only meant for a first run and denied criticism caused the removal; Apple pulled them from YouTube and apple.com on 22 Aug 2012. Counterpoint: YouGov argued they may have suited the older Olympic prime-time audience. — [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2012/08/07/apple-stops-airing-genius-ads-on-olympic-television-broadcasts/) [A]; [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2012/08/22/apple-pulls-genius-tv-ads-from-youtube-and-apple-com/) [A]; [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/2725387/apple-s-biggest-fans-are-older-consumers.html) [T]
- **"Relax, it's iPhone" OOH**: a toddler billboard in Italy drew a complaint and Apple agreed to replace it. — [Gizchina](https://www.gizchina.com/apple/apples-relax-its-iphone-campaign-is-not-so-relaxed-anymore) [B]
- **"1984" near-failure**: Apple's board hated it and tried to sell the airtime. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/29867/how-apples-1984-ad-was-almost-canceled) [T]

### Inferences
- Lessons (analyst interpretation): (1) do not make the product the destroyer of things the audience loves; frame it as an addition to human craft; (2) never advertise a capability before it ships, especially with a demo-style spot; (3) do not make the user (or a helper figure) look stupid in order to make the product look smart ("Genius", Apple Intelligence tone); (4) long-form comedy shot abroad needs local review of how the place is portrayed; (5) System1's reversed-"Crush!" test (1.8 vs 1.9) suggests the problem was the premise, not the edit order.
- Internal dislike is not a predictor of failure ("1984", and "Think Different" per Siltanen), so the lessons above concern audience harm and truthfulness, not boldness.

### Gaps
- Whether Apple changed its approval process after "Crush!" is discussed in the Fast Company Myhren interview, which could not be read.
- Individual titles within the 2012 "Genius" series were not confirmed.
- One search extract attached the "missed the mark" wording to the Thai apology as well; that attribution looked muddled, so only the "Crush!" attribution is reported here.

Hard third-party effectiveness data on Apple ads is scarce; the best points are a handful of System1 scores (low for iPad Pro work, high for the Underdogs' "The Invincibles" and for a holiday ad), award results (Grand Effie 2007, Cannes Grand Prix 2015 Outdoor and 2025 Creative Effectiveness for Shot on iPhone), and self-reported YouTube views of 30M+ for the long Underdogs films.
